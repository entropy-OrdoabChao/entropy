# Case Type: FOIA Violation

## Classification Keywords
foia, freedom of information, privacy act, 552, 552a, constructive denial, records request

## COUNT_1
title: VIOLATION OF THE FREEDOM OF INFORMATION ACT
statute: 5 U.S.C. 552
controlling_law:
- 5 U.S.C. 552(a)(6)(A)(i): Agency S{{JUDGE}} make determination within 20 working days
- 5 U.S.C. 552(a)(4)(B): Court S{{JUDGE}} order production of improperly withheld records
- 5 U.S.C. 552(a)(6)(C): Failure to comply = constructive denial = exhaustion
duties:
- text: Agency S{{JUDGE}} make determination on FOIA request within 20 working days
  citation: 552(a)(6)(A)(i)
- text: Agency S{{JUDGE}} notify requester of any extension under unusual circumstances
  citation: 552(a)(6)(B)
- text: Agency S{{JUDGE}} produce all non-exempt responsive records
  citation: 552(a)(3)(A)
elements:
- desc: Plaintiff made a proper FOIA request to an agency
  if: Plaintiff submitted a written request reasonably describing records to an agency subject to FOIA
  and: Agency acknowledged receipt and assigned tracking number
- desc: Agency improperly withheld agency records
  if: Agency must produce non-exempt responsive records within statutory timeframe
  and: Agency produced zero records and claimed no exemption
- desc: Requester exhausted administrative remedies
  if: Administrative remedies are exhausted when agency fails to comply with time limits
  and: Agency failed to respond within 20 working days and requester filed appeal
relief:
- Injunctive relief ordering immediate production of all responsive records within 14 days
- Vaughn index for any records withheld under claimed exemptions
- Attorney fees and litigation costs under 5 U.S.C. 552(a)(4)(E)
- Retained jurisdiction until full compliance
admission: Defendant, through Associate General Counsel, admitted constructive denial on the record
breach_duty: 5 U.S.C. 552(a)(6)(A)(i) -- 20-working-day determination requirement
breach_conduct: Defendants failed to issue any determination within 20 working days, failed to invoke any extension, and produced zero records
decision_trigger:
  if: mandatory duties apply under 552(a)(6)(A)(i)
  and: all prima facie elements are satisfied AND Defendant admits non-compliance
  then: Court S{{JUDGE}} order production of improperly withheld records under 552(a)(4)(B)

## COUNT_2
title: VIOLATION OF THE PRIVACY ACT OF 1974
statute: 5 U.S.C. 552a
controlling_law:
- 5 U.S.C. 552a(d)(1): Agency S{{JUDGE}} permit individual access to records pertaining to him
- 5 U.S.C. 552a(g)(1)(B): Court jurisdiction over refusal to provide access
- 5 U.S.C. 552a(g)(4)(A): If intentional or willful, United States S{{JUDGE}} be liable -- minimum $1,000
duties:
- text: Agency S{{JUDGE}} permit Plaintiff access to records about himself
  citation: 552a(d)(1)
- text: Agency S{{JUDGE}} maintain records with accuracy, relevance, timeliness, and completeness
  citation: 552a(e)(5)
elements:
- desc: Records pertain to Plaintiff and are maintained in a system of records
  if: Records about Plaintiff are maintained by agency in systems of records
  and: Plaintiff is the named subject and submitted verified identity under penalty of perjury
- desc: Agency refused to provide access
  if: Agency must permit access to first-party records upon verified request
  and: Agency produced zero records despite confirmation that records exist and are producible
- desc: Refusal was intentional or willful
  if: Intentional or willful conduct requires deliberate indifference or conscious disregard of statutory duties
  and: Agency acknowledged request, missed deadline, confirmed violation, demanded already-submitted verification after deadline. Such conduct constitutes reckless disregard of statutory duties
relief:
- Actual damages pursuant to 5 U.S.C. 552a(g)(4)(A) in amount to be determined at trial
- Statutory minimum damages of $1,000
- Order compelling immediate access to all Privacy Act records
breach_duty: 5 U.S.C. 552a(d)(1) -- right of access to first-party records
breach_conduct: Refused to provide access to records about Plaintiff for over 100 days despite confirmed violation
decision_trigger:
  if: records pertain to Plaintiff in a system of records
  and: access was refused AND conduct was intentional or willful
  then: United States S{{JUDGE}} be liable for actual damages, minimum $1,000 under 552a(g)(4)(A)

## COUNT_3
title: MANDAMUS
statute: 28 U.S.C. 1361
controlling_law:
- 28 U.S.C. 1361: District courts have original jurisdiction over mandamus actions to compel federal officers to perform duties owed to the plaintiff
- 5 U.S.C. 552(a)(6)(A)(i): Agency S{{JUDGE}} determine within 20 working days (non-discretionary)
- 5 U.S.C. 552a(d)(1): Agency S{{JUDGE}} permit access (non-discretionary)
duties:
- text: Named officials have non-discretionary ministerial duty to process and produce responsive records within statutory timeframe
  citation: 552(a)(6)(A)(i), 552a(d)(1)
elements:
- desc: Plaintiff has a clear right to the relief sought
  if: FOIA and Privacy Act create enforceable rights to records production
  and: Plaintiff submitted a proper verified request
- desc: Defendants have a clear non-discretionary duty to act
  if: Duty to respond within 20 working days and provide first-party access is mandatory
  and: Named Defendants are the specific officials assigned to or responsible for processing this request
- desc: No adequate alternative remedy exists
  if: Administrative appeal has been exhausted AND resulted in remand with no production
  and: Over 40 days have elapsed since remand with no compliance AND responsible officials email accounts reject correspondence
relief:
- Writ of mandamus compelling Defendants to immediately process and produce all responsive records
- Order requiring access to all Privacy Act records within 14 days
- Court-supervised compliance with periodic reporting until full production
breach_duty: Non-discretionary duties under 552(a)(6)(A)(i) and 552a(d)(1)
breach_conduct: Named officials failed to perform ministerial duties for over 100 days despite internal confirmation of constructive denial
decision_trigger:
  if: Plaintiff has clear right, Defendants have clear non-discretionary duty, and no adequate alternative exists
  and: Defendants have failed to perform for over 100 days despite internal confirmation of constructive denial
  then: Court S{{JUDGE}} issue a writ of mandamus compelling immediate compliance
