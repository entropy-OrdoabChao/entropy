## The Action Is Not Frivolous

Under 28 U.S.C. § 1915(e)(2)(B), a court shall dismiss an IFP case only if the action
"(i) is frivolous or malicious; (ii) fails to state a claim on which relief may be
granted; or (iii) seeks monetary relief against a defendant who is immune from such
relief." The Supreme Court has held that a complaint is frivolous only when it "lacks an
arguable basis either in law or in fact." *Neitzke v. Williams*, 490 U.S. 319, 325 (1989).

Plaintiff's claims are grounded in specific statutory violations with identified
defendants and concrete injuries. The claims have an arguable basis in both law and
fact, and therefore do not meet the frivolity threshold under § 1915(e)(2).
