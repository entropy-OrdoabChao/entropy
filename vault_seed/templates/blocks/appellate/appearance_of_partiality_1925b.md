## APPEARANCE OF PARTIALITY — THE RECORD'S OWN PATTERN

The party does not impugn the court's motives and need not. *"A fair trial in a fair tribunal is a
basic requirement of due process"* (*In re Murchison*, 349 U.S. 133, 136 (1955)). Disqualification is
required — **without any proof of actual bias** — where *"the probability of actual bias ... is too
high to be constitutionally tolerable"* (*Caperton v. A.T. Massey Coal Co.*, 556 U.S. 868, 872 (2009)),
or where the court's *"impartiality might reasonably be questioned,"* including where the judge *"as a
fiduciary ... has a financial interest in the subject matter in controversy"* ({{j_disqualification}}). The standard is objective: an appearance of partiality arises *"even though no actual
partiality exists ... so long as the public might reasonably believe"* it (*Liljeberg v. Health Services
Acquisition Corp.*, 486 U.S. 847 (1988)); and a decisionmaker with a *"direct, personal, substantial,
pecuniary interest"* in the outcome is disqualified (*Tumey v. Ohio*, 273 U.S. 510 (1927)).
