## PRESERVATION AND RESERVATION OF RIGHTS

Appellant **waives nothing, and files this Statement to make that clear of record.** No silence,
omission, or compliance with the court's order is, or shall be construed as, a waiver — waiver being
the *intentional relinquishment of a known right* (*Johnson v. Zerbst*, 304 U.S. 458 (1938)).

**The waiver invoked against Appellant is a RULE, and a rule must apply equally.** The waiver that
threatens this appeal is Pa.R.A.P. 1925(b)(4)(vii) — a *rule of court*, not a statute of the General
Assembly. A standard the court imposes on one party but excuses in itself or in its favored parties is
**wanting in mutuality and therefore void**; there is no such thing as a unilateral agreement. Equity
forbids the asymmetry: **"Equality is equity,"** and **"he who seeks equity must do equity"**; a party
may not **approbate and reprobate** — take the benefit of a rule while repudiating its burden.

**Applied to the garnishees — by the court's own waiver standard, and by the Rules.** Garnishee State
Farm, having failed to answer the interrogatories in garnishment within the **twenty days** required by
Pa.R.C.P. 3144(b), is **subject to default judgment** (Pa.R.C.P. 3146(a)); Garnishee Example Trust Co,
having filed an **unverified** answer, stands on a defective pleading whose averments are **deemed
admitted** (Pa.R.C.P. 1024; 1029(b)).
The court may not enforce waiver-by-deadline against Appellant while excusing the garnishees' own
noncompliance with the same deadlines and the same Rules.

**Applied to the court — the same reciprocal standard.** The court's duties are mandatory, not
discretionary, and are not excused: Pa.R.A.P. 1925(a) **mandates** a written opinion stating the reasons
for the order; the judicial oath (Pa. Const. art. VI, § 3) and the Code of Judicial Conduct — Rules 2.2
(upholding the law impartially), 2.6 (the right to be heard), 2.9 (ex parte communications), and 2.11
(disqualification for economic interest, *Caperton v. A.T. Massey Coal Co.*, 556 U.S. 868 (2009)) — bind
the court as its Rules bind Appellant. Judicial immunity protects only *judicial acts taken with
jurisdiction*; it does **not** reach non-judicial acts or acts taken in the **clear absence of all
jurisdiction** (*Stump v. Sparkman*, 435 U.S. 349 (1978); *Bradley v. Fisher*, 80 U.S. (13 Wall.) 335
(1871)), and it does **not** bar prospective injunctive or declaratory relief (*Pulliam v. Allen*, 466
U.S. 522 (1984)). After a notice of appeal, Pa.R.A.P. 1701(b) fixes the court's remaining authority;
relief entered outside that carve-out is entered in the absence of jurisdiction.

**Constitutional deprivation — reserved.** A final money judgment is vested property. Impairing
Appellant's right to collect that judgment — and enjoining its collection — without notice or hearing
**deprives Appellant of property, and takes it,** contrary to the **Fifth Amendment** (due process of
law; the taking of property without just compensation) and **Pa. Const. art. I, §§ 1, 9, and 11**
(the right to acquire and protect property, due process, and the guarantee that every person shall have
a remedy by due course of law). Appellant reserves this claim and **every remedy not barred by immunity**
— declaratory and prospective relief, recusal, mandamus, review by the Superior Court, and referral to
the Judicial Conduct Board (Pa. Const. art. V, § 18) — and does not, by filing this Statement, consent
to, ratify, or waive any of them.
