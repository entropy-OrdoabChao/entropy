## Grounds for Extension of Time Under FRCP 6(b)(1)(A)

### A. The Standard — Good Cause After Deadline

Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 6(b)(1)(A) provides that the court may extend the time for any act "for good cause" — and **unlike the excusable neglect standard of (B), the good cause standard applies even after the deadline has expired.** The Third Circuit has held that the good cause standard is more lenient than excusable neglect and applies when the court or a rule sets the time limit.

For pro se litigants, the standard of review is especially lenient. Under *Haines v. Kerner*, 404 U.S. 519 (1972), pro se filings must be construed liberally. Under *Erickson v. Pardus*, 551 U.S. 89 (2007), pro se complaints are held to a lesser standard than those drafted by counsel.

### B. Good Cause Exists Here

Good cause exists here for the following reasons:

1. **Pro se status** — Plaintiff is not represented by counsel and is navigating complex procedural requirements without legal assistance.
2. **Complexity of the case** — The case involves multiple defendants across multiple jurisdictions with overlapping federal and state claims (FCRA, FDCPA, UTPCPL), requiring careful attention to each defendant's individual procedural posture.
3. **No prejudice to opposing parties** — Granting the extension would cause no prejudice to any opposing party, who have had ample time to respond to the underlying claims and have filed their answers.
4. **No bad faith** — There is no evidence of bad faith or intentional delay. The missed deadline reflects the difficulty of managing multiple claims against multiple defendants without counsel.

**IF** a pro se litigant has missed a deadline due to the complexity of the case and the difficulty of navigating multiple procedural requirements without counsel, AND granting the extension would cause no prejudice to opposing parties, AND there is no evidence of bad faith or intentional delay, THEN the court must grant the extension for good cause under FRCP 6(b)(1)(A).
