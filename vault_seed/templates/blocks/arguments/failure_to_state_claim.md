## Failure to State a Claim Upon Which Relief Can Be Granted

A pleading must contain "a short and plain statement of the claim
showing that the pleader is entitled to relief." FRCP 8(a)(2). To
survive review, factual allegations must be enough to raise a right
to relief above the speculative level. *Bell Atl. Corp. v. Twombly*,
550 U.S. 544, 555 (2007); *Ashcroft v. Iqbal*, 556 U.S. 662, 678 (2009).

When a dismissal is challenged, the trial court must apply the standard set forth in *Haines v. Kerner*, 404 U.S. 519 (1972), which requires that dismissal be proper only if "no set of facts could entitle the plaintiff to relief." This standard requires the court to evaluate whether the complaint's allegations, taken as true, could support a valid claim -- not whether the plaintiff will ultimately prevail.

Where the dismissal exceeds this standard or ignores the plaintiff's factual allegations, the proper remedy is to vacate the dismissal under FRCP 12(b) (or Pa.R.C.P. 1031) and remand for reconsideration consistent with *Haines*.
