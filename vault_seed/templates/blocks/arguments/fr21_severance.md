## Severance Under FRCP 21

### A. The Standard — FRCP 21

Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 21 provides that misjoinder of parties is not a ground for dismissing an action. On motion or on its own, the court may at any time, on just terms, add or drop a party. The court may also sever any claim against a party.

### B. The Standard — FRCP 42 — Consolidation and Separate Trials

Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 42 provides:

- **42(a)** — When actions involving a common question of law or fact are pending, the court may consolidate them for hearing or trial.
- **42(b)** — The court may order separate trials of any claim, cross-claim, counterclaim, or third-party claim when severance promotes convenience, clarity, or judicial economy.

### C. Application — Why Severance Is Required

The defects identified above — particularly the misjoinder defects and the construction defects — demonstrate that the claims against certain parties have been improperly joined with other claims that present separate and distinct legal issues. The proper remedy is severance under FRCP 21 and FRCP 42(b).

The court may not dismiss an action for misjoinder under FRCP 21 — it must instead sever the improperly joined claims and allow the remaining claims to proceed.
