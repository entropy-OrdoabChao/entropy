## Relief from Judgment Under FRCP 60(b)

### A. The Standard — FRCP 60(b)

Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 60(b) provides six grounds for relief from a final judgment or order:

- **(b)(1)** — Mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect
- **(b)(2)** — Newly discovered evidence that could not have been discovered earlier with due diligence
- **(b)(3)** — Fraud, misrepresentation, or misconduct by an opposing party
- **(b)(4)** — The judgment or order is void
- **(b)(5)** — The judgment or order has been satisfied, released, or discharged, or it is no longer equitable that the judgment should have prospective application
- **(b)(6)** — Any other reason that justifies relief (catch-all)

**Crucially, FRCP 60(b)(4) has no time limitation** — a void judgment can be challenged at any time. As the Supreme Court held in *United Student Aid Funds v. Espinosa*, 559 U.S. 260, 269 (2010), a judgment is void under (b)(4) when the court lacked jurisdiction over the parties or the subject matter.

### B. Grounds for Relief — Void Judgment Under FRCP 60(b)(4)

The judgment/order at issue is void because it rests on factual findings that are directly contradicted by the court's own docket record. Where a court's finding contradicts the record it maintains, the judgment lacks the foundation of factual accuracy and is void ab initio.

**IF** the court's judgment or order rests on factual findings contradicted by the court's own docket record, AND no competent rebuttal evidence exists, THEN the judgment is void under FRCP 60(b)(4) and must be vacated.

### C. Grounds for Relief — Fraud Under FRCP 60(b)(3)

In the alternative, the judgment/order is void because it rests on fraudulent factual findings — the assertion that a party was not served when proof of service is filed of record. As *Hazel-Atlas* established, this constitutes fraud on the court itself.

**IF** the court's judgment or order relies on a factual finding that is contradicted by filed proof of service, THEN the judgment is void under FRCP 60(b)(3) for fraud and must be vacated.

### D. One-Year Clock — FRCP 60(b)(1)-(3)

For FRCP 60(b)(1)-(3) grounds, the one-year clock runs from the date of the judgment/order. Under FRCP 60(c)(1), a motion under (b)(1)-(3) must be made within one year after the judgment or order.
