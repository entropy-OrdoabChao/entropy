## EXCEPTIONAL CIRCUMSTANCES JUSTIFYING RECALL

### 1. The Mandate Issued on a Premise Contradicted by the Record

The Court's dismissal order rested on the finding that the appellant "failed to file a brief." This finding is contradicted by the record: the appellant filed a 52-page opening brief on 2025-11-08 via USPS Certified Mail (tracking 9214890142980433309153, delivered and signed for at the Clerk's Office) and via CM/ECF. The mandate issued on a factual premise the record does not support.

### 2. The Court Never Issued a Briefing Schedule

The Court represented that it would schedule a briefing and never did. The docket reflects only that a briefing date was "to be issued." Without a scheduling order, there was no deadline for the appellant to violate. The dismissal for "failure to file brief" therefore lacks a predicate — the Court cannot hold a party to a deadline that never existed.

### 3. No Appellant Fault

The appellant acted diligently by filing the brief via both certified mail and CM/ECF. The failure to recognize the brief's timely filing was a clerical/administrative error on the Court's part. There is no evidence of appellant neglect or intentional delay.

### 4. Manifest Injustice

If the mandate is not recalled, the appellant's right to have the appeal decided on the merits will be lost due to a clerical error — not due to any fault of the appellant. This is the precise circumstance that justifies recall under Calderon v. Thompson.

### 5. Relief Requested

Plaintiff respectfully requests that the Court:
1. Recall its mandate (or certified order in lieu of mandate);
2. Reinstate the appeal to active status;
3. Issue a briefing schedule;
4. Grant such other relief as the Court deems just.
