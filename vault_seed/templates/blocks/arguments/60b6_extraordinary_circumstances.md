## Extraordinary Circumstances Under FRCP 60(b)(6)

### A. The Standard — "Extraordinary Circumstances"

FRCP 60(b)(6) is a catch-all provision for "any other reason that justifies relief" not covered by subdivisions (b)(1)-(5). The Supreme Court has held in *Ackermann v. United States*, 340 U.S. 193 (1950), that the circumstances must be truly **extraordinary** — not merely inconvenient, surprising, or unfavorable.

The Third Circuit has consistently applied this strict standard: (b)(6) is not a "get-out-of-jail-free" card. It applies only where:
1. The circumstances are extraordinary and compelling;
2. No other subdivision provides adequate relief; and
3. The movant was not at fault in failing to discover the grounds earlier.

**Unlike (b)(4), (b)(6) has a ONE-YEAR LIMIT** under FRCP 60(c)(1). The motion must be filed within one year after the judgment or order.

### D. (b)(6) Cannot Circumvent Other Subdivisions

Per *Dietz v. Kollmorgen Corp.*, 579 U.S. 253 (2016), a party cannot use (b)(6) to circumvent the specific requirements of other subdivisions. If the ground fits within (b)(1)-(5), that subdivision controls. (b)(6) is truly a last resort — for circumstances that are extraordinary, compelling, and genuinely outside the scope of the other subdivisions.
