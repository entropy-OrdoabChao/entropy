## ARGUMENT FOR COMPELLING A BRIEFING SCHEDULE

### 1. The Clerk's Mandatory Duty Under FRAP 31

FRAP 31(a) commands: "The clerk shall promptly schedule the case for briefing and oral argument." FRAP 31(c) requires the clerk to "notify all parties of the dates for filing briefs and for oral argument." These are mandatory duties — not discretionary. The clerk cannot skip this step.

### 2. No Deadline Can Bind Without a Scheduling Order

The court's dismissal for "failure to file brief" presupposes a deadline existed. But no deadline can exist where no scheduling order was ever issued. The Third Circuit's own Local Appellate Rule 107.2(a) requires briefing to conform to the clerk's scheduling order. Where no scheduling order was issued, there is no deadline to violate.

### 3. Application to This Case

The appellant filed a 52-page opening brief on 2025-11-08 via USPS Certified Mail (tracking 9214890142980433309153, delivered and signed for at the Clerk's Office, 601 Market St, Room 2609) and via CM/ECF. The Court never issued a briefing schedule. The dismissal entered on 2026-05-15 for "failure to file brief" therefore rests on a premise contradicted by the record: a deadline that never existed.

### 4. Relief Requested

Plaintiff respectfully requests that the Court:
1. Compel the Clerk to issue a briefing schedule;
2. Correct the record to reflect that the appellant's brief was timely filed and delivered;
3. Reinstate the appeal to active status.
