## Defendants Rely on an Order Whose Validity Is Actively Under Challenge

Defendants' filings cite -- and ask this Court to give weight to -- an order
issued in a related federal proceeding involving these same Plaintiffs and
parties in privity. That order is **the subject of a pending Petition for
Writ of Mandamus** in the United States Court of Appeals for the Third
Circuit. Defendants neither disclosed the pendency of that mandamus
challenge nor acknowledged that the cited order's validity is in active
appellate dispute.

Reliance on a contested order, without disclosure, is structurally
defective for two independent reasons:

**First**, an order subject to mandamus review on jurisdictional grounds
may be *void ab initio* -- not merely voidable. *See* FRCP 60(b)(4);
28 U.S.C. § 1651. A void order has no precedential force in any
proceeding, related or unrelated.

**Second**, even setting aside voidness, the failure to disclose the
pendency of the mandamus petition deprived this Court of material
information needed to assess the cited authority's weight. Defendants
cannot cherry-pick the favorable order while concealing the very
challenge that would, if successful, eliminate it from the record.

Plaintiff respectfully requests that this Court (i) STRIKE all citations
to and reliance upon the contested order, (ii) decline to give the order
any weight in pending or future rulings until the Third Circuit
disposes of the mandamus petition, and (iii) consider the candor
implications addressed below.
