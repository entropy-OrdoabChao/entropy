## GROUNDS FOR REINSTATEMENT

### 1. Standard

An appeal dismissed for failure to prosecute may be reinstated upon a showing of good cause. *See* Fed. R. App. P. 3(a); 3d Cir. L.A.R. 107.2(b). A dismissal under L.A.R. 107.2(b) is administrative in character, and the Court retains authority to reinstate where the premise of the dismissal is not borne out by the record. Reinstatement is particularly appropriate where the appellant did in fact comply with the obligation the dismissal found unmet, or where any non-compliance is attributable to excusable neglect rather than disregard of the Court's orders. *See* Fed. R. App. P. 26(b); *Poulis v. State Farm Fire & Cas. Co.*, 747 F.2d 863 (3d Cir. 1984) (dismissal for failure to prosecute disfavored absent a showing of fault and prejudice).

### 4. Conclusion

Because the dismissal rests on a premise contradicted by the record, and
because Plaintiff has at all times sought to prosecute this appeal, good
cause exists to reinstate the appeal and to issue (or re-issue) a briefing
schedule so the appeal may proceed to a merits determination.
