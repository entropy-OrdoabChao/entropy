# 12(c) Standard Identical to Summary Judgment

The standard for judgment on the pleadings under Fed. R. Civ. P. 12(c) is identical to the standard for summary judgment under Fed. R. Civ. P. 56. Fed. R. Civ. P. 12(c). *Celotex Corp. v. Catrett*, 477 U.S. 317, 322 (1986); *Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc.*, 477 U.S. 242, 247-48 (1986); *Matsushita Elec. Indus. Co. v. Zenith Radio Corp.*, 475 U.S. 574, 587 (1986). Judgment is proper only if "no genuine dispute exists as to any material fact and the movant is entitled to judgment as a matter of law." Fed. R. Civ. P. 56(a).

The moving party bears the initial burden of demonstrating the absence of a genuine dispute of material fact. *Celotex*, 477 U.S. at 323. Once that burden is met, the non-moving party must set forth specific facts showing a genuine dispute for trial. *Celotex*, 477 U.S. at 324. A dispute is genuine if the evidence is such that a reasonable jury could return a verdict for the non-moving party. *Anderson*, 477 U.S. at 248.

The court's inquiry is limited to the pleadings and matters of public record — no extrinsic evidence may be considered. *Celotex*, 477 U.S. at 324 n.2; *Harrington v. Town of South Berwick*, 574 F.3d 65, 70 (1st Cir. 2009). If the court considers extrinsic materials, the motion must be treated as one for summary judgment under Fed. R. Civ. P. 56, and the parties must be given a reasonable opportunity to present all pertinent material. Fed. R. Civ. P. 56(c).

The court must draw all reasonable inferences in favor of the non-moving party. *Celotex*, 477 U.S. at 324; *Anderson*, 477 U.S. at 255. The court does not weigh the evidence or make credibility determinations. *Anderson*, 477 U.S. at 255.

Because the standard is identical to summary judgment, the same principles apply: the court must determine whether there is a genuine dispute of material fact, and the non-moving party is entitled to have the court draw all reasonable inferences in its favor.
