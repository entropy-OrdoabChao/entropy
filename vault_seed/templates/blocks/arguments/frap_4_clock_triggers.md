## FRAP 4(a)(1) APPEAL CLOCK TRIGGERS

### The Rule

FRAP 4(a)(1): In a civil case, the notice of appeal must be filed within 30 days after entry of the judgment or order appealed from.

### Trigger 1: 54(b) Certification Granted

If the district court grants 54(b) certification, the 30-day clock starts from the date of the certification ORDER. The plaintiff must file a Notice of Appeal within 30 days of that certification.

**This is the primary trigger for this case.** Example Card Issuer was dismissed with prejudice (ECF 122). If the court grants 54(b) certification, the clock starts from that certification date.

### Trigger 2: Global Final Judgment

If no 54(b) certification is ever granted, the clock starts from the entry of global final judgment — i.e., when ALL claims against ALL defendants are resolved. This is the fallback trigger.

### Trigger 3: FRAP 4(a)(3) — Extension for Excusable Neglect

If the 30-day period expires, the district court may extend the time for excusable neglect or good cause. This is NOT a right — it is discretionary.

### Critical Timing Points for This Case

1. **ECF 122 (09/19/2025)**: Example Card Issuer dismissed with prejudice. This is the order that needs 54(b) certification.
2. **If 54(b) certified**: 30 days from certification order → file Notice of Appeal
3. **If 54(b) denied and 1292(b) certified**: 10 days from 1292(b) certification → petition court of appeals
4. **If global final judgment enters**: 30 days from final judgment → file Notice of Appeal

### Preservation Requirement

The Notice of Appeal must:
- Identify the judgment or order appealed from
- Name the party or parties taking the appeal
- Specify the case number
- Be filed with the district clerk

Failure to file a timely Notice of Appeal = loss of appellate jurisdiction.
