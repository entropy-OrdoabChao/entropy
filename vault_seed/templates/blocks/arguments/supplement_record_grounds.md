## GROUNDS TO SUPPLEMENT THE RECORD

### 1. Legal Standard

FRAP 10(e) provides: "On appeal the record should be corrected if there is any difference between what actually occurred and what the reporter's transcription shows." This authority extends to supplementing the record with documents that should be part of it but were not — such as a USPS signed receipt proving delivery of a brief. The court has inherent authority to maintain an accurate record.

### 2. The Record Is Incomplete

The appellate record does not contain the USPS signed receipt for tracking number 9214890142980433309153, which proves the appellant's brief was delivered to and signed for by the Clerk's Office on 2025-11-08. Without this document, the record is incomplete and the Court cannot determine whether the brief was actually received.

### 3. Application

The appellant's brief was filed on 2025-11-08 via both USPS Certified Mail (tracking 9214890142980433309153) and CM/ECF. The USPS signed receipt is critical evidence that the brief was delivered. The record should contain this document. FRAP 10(e) requires the record to be corrected when it does not reflect what actually occurred.

### 4. Relief Requested

Plaintiff respectfully requests that the Court:
1. Supplement the record with the USPS signed receipt (Exhibit G);
2. Note on the record that the appellant's brief was delivered and signed for by the Clerk's Office;
3. Reinstate the appeal based on the corrected record.
