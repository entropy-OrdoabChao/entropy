## Fraud on the Court Under FRCP 60(d)(3)

### The Standard — FRCP 60(d)(3) and Controlling Authority

Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 60(d)(3) permits relief from a final judgment or order when "the judgment or order is based on fraud, misrepresentation, or misconduct by an opposing party." Critically, **no time limitation applies** — unlike the one-year clock for FRCP 60(b)(1)-(5).

As the Supreme Court explained in *Hazel-Atlas Glass Co. v. Hartford-Empire Co.*, 322 U.S. 238, 246 (1944), fraud on the court is "the ultimate injustice — a fraud on the judicial system itself and not the mere fraud of one party against another." The Third Circuit has applied this principle in *Herring v. United States*, 424 F.3d 384, 391 (3d Cir. 2005), holding that relief under FRCP 60(d)(3) is available where the court's order rests on a factual finding contradicted by the court's own record.

### Application — The Record Contradicts the Order

The Order at Dkt {{latest_order.dkt_no}} rests on factual findings that are directly contradicted by the filed docket record. Where a proof of service is filed of record under FRCP 4(l), the docket itself establishes service unless rebutted by competent evidence. No such rebuttal exists here.

**IF** the court's order contains a factual finding that is directly contradicted by a document filed of record in the same case, AND no competent rebuttal evidence exists, THEN the finding constitutes fraud on the court under FRCP 60(d)(3) and the order must be vacated.

### Authority

*Hazel-Atlas Glass Co. v. Hartford-Empire Co.*, 322 U.S. 238 (1944); *Herring v. United States*, 424 F.3d 384 (3d Cir. 2005); Fed. R. Civ. P. 60(d)(3), 60(c)(1); Fed. R. Civ. P. 4(l); Fed. R. Evid. 201(b)(2).
