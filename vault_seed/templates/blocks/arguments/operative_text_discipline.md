## The Operative-Text Discipline

### A. The Rule: Quote First, Construct Second

**IF** the statute or regulation defines a term with "means" — the definition is EXHAUSTIVE. Nothing outside the definition is within the scope. No expansion by implication.

**IF** the statute or regulation defines a term with "includes" — the definition is NOT automatically expansive. The statute supplies its own construction rule:
  - **IF** the statute carries an explicit expansion clause (e.g., 26 U.S.C. § 7701(c) — applies only to definitions CONTAINED IN THIS TITLE), QUOTE the clause and note its bounded scope: the expansion applies only to definitions contained within the same title.
  - **IF** the statute has NO expansion clause, the default canons govern: EJUSDEM GENERIS (general words following an enumeration are limited to the same class) and EXPRESSIO UNIUS (expression of one thing excludes others) — the ENUMERATION DEFINES THE CLASS.
  - Never wire a blanket includes=expansive rule. The statute dictates the construction.

**IF** a definition hangs on another defined term, trace the chain link by link. QUOTE each link. Flag any mid-chain swap of the defined term for its common-word homonym as a citable defect. The chain must stay inside the SAME statute unless a cross-reference imports it.

### B. Application: The Operative Word Controls

**IF** the opponent renders a definition by silently adding a word or deleting a limiter,
**AND** the added word or deleted limiter changes the scope of the defined term,
**THEN** the rendering is a facial departure from the statutory text.

Every silently inserted word (e.g., "only") or deleted limiter is a facial departure. This cuts BOTH ways: an opponent silently EXPANDING an enumerated definition without an expansion clause is the same defect as silently SHRINKING one.

### C. Authority: Candor Hooks

The opponent's failure to quote the verbatim text and flag the departure violates:
- **Pa.R.P.C. 3.3** — Candor toward tribunal (no false statements of law)
- **FRCP 11(b)(2)** — Certification that legal contentions are warranted by existing law
- **28 U.S.C. § 1927** — Counsel liability for excessive costs

### D. Gould v. Gould Principle

> **Gould v. Gould, 245 U.S. 151, 153 (1917):** "The rule of strict construction against the government in taxing provisions is well established. Doubt is construed against the government, in favor of the citizen."

This principle extends beyond taxing provisions: when a statute defines a term, the definition controls. Nothing outside the definition is within the scope, and no canon may be invoked to expand beyond the text without a statutory expansion clause.
