## GROUNDS TO STAY THE MANDATE

### 1. Legal Standard

FRAP 41(b)(1) provides: "A party may move to stay the mandate pending a petition for rehearing. The stay may not exceed 14 days unless the court extends it. A party must file a notice of appeal to the Supreme Court within the time for filing a petition for rehearing measured from the date the mandate issues."

The mandate issues 7 days after entry of judgment unless the court orders otherwise (FRAP 41(a)). A stay prevents the mandate from issuing, preserving jurisdiction for a petition for rehearing or other post-dismissal relief.

### 2. Distinction Between Stay and Recall

A STAY (FRAP 41(b)) prevents the mandate from issuing. A RECALL (FRAP 41(d)) withdraws an already-issued mandate. This motion is appropriate because the mandate has NOT yet issued (or its issuance is imminent). If the mandate has already issued, the appellant must file a motion to recall under FRAP 41(d) instead.

### 3. Application

The appeal was dismissed on 2026-05-15 (dkt 54). The mandate may issue as early as 7 days after entry of the dismissal order. The appellant intends to file a petition for panel rehearing under FRAP 40. A stay of the mandate pending that petition is appropriate because:
- The dismissal rests on a premise contradicted by the record (brief was filed);
- The petition for rehearing raises questions of exceptional importance;
- Without a stay, the mandate will issue and the dismissal will become final before the petition can be considered.

### 4. Relief Requested

Plaintiff respectfully requests that the Court:
1. Stay the mandate pending the filing and disposition of a petition for panel rehearing;
2. Set a duration of 30 days (or such other period as the Court deems appropriate);
3. Require the appellant to file the petition for rehearing within the stay period.
