## IFP EXTINGUISHES THE APPELLATE FEE REQUIREMENT

### 1. The Premise Contradicted by the Record

The Clerk dismissed this appeal for "failure to pay the requisite fee as directed."
Yet the District Court GRANTED in forma pauperis status on {{ifp_grant_date}}
(ECF No. {{ifp_grant_dkt}}). Under 28 U.S.C. § 1915(a), a party granted IFP
proceeds "without prepaying fees or providing security for costs." The fee
requirement the Clerk enforced was already extinguished by the district court's
IFP grant. [corpus: 28 U.S.C. 1915(a); 3d Cir. L.A.R. 107.1(a)]

### 2. The Clerk's Duty Upon IFP Grant

Under 3d Cir. L.A.R. 107.1(a), the docketing fee ($605 for appeals filed on/after
2023-12-01) is payable "unless the appellant proceeds in forma pauperis."
Once the district court grants IFP on the notice of appeal, the appellate clerk
must accept the appeal WITHOUT demanding the fee. The clerk's demand for the fee
— and the subsequent dismissal for non-payment — rests on a premise the record
contradicts. [corpus: 3d Cir. L.A.R. 107.1(a)]

### 3. Timeliness of the IFP Response

The IFP response was mailed to the Third Circuit Clerk via LetterStream job
{{letterstream_job}} on {{mail_date}}, completing {{completion_date}}.
The dismissal order was entered {{order_date}} at {{order_time}} — while the
response was in transit. The appellant's diligent prosecution is documented by
the mailing record. [doc: LetterStream job {{letterstream_job}}]

### 4. Good Cause for Reinstatement

Under 3d Cir. L.A.R. 107.2(b), a dismissal for failure to prosecute is
administrative in character, and the Court retains authority to reinstate where
the premise of the dismissal is not borne out by the record. Here, the premise
—"failure to pay the requisite fee"—is contradicted by the district court's IFP
grant, which eliminated the fee requirement entirely. The dismissal is therefore
premised on a procedural error, not on the appellant's neglect. [corpus: 3d Cir.
L.A.R. 107.2(b); Poulis v. State Farm Fire & Cas. Co., 747 F.2d 863 (3d Cir. 1984)]
