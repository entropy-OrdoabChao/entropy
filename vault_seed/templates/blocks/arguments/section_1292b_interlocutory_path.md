# Section 1292(b) Interlocutory Appeal Path

**Authority:** 28 U.S.C. § 1292(b) + Cohen v. Beneficial Industrial Loan Corp., 337 U.S. 541 (1949)
**Supporting:** Cheney v. U.S. Dist. Court for D.C., 542 U.S. 367 (2004)
**Binding on EDPA:** Yes — Supreme Court + 3rd Circuit precedent

## The Collateral-Order / Interlocutory Appeal Framework

When FRCP 54(b) certification fails (or the court refuses to act), the party has alternative paths to appellate review:

### Path 1: 28 U.S.C. § 1292(b) — Interlocutory Appeal

> "When a district judge, in making in a civil action an order not otherwise appealable under this section, shall be of the opinion that such order involves a controlling question of law as to which there is substantial ground for difference of opinion and that an immediate appeal from the order may materially advance the ultimate termination of the litigation, he shall so state in writing in such order." — 28 U.S.C. § 1292(b)

**Requirements:**
1. The order must involve a "controlling question of law"
2. There must be "substantial ground for difference of opinion"
3. An immediate appeal must "materially advance the ultimate termination of the litigation"
4. The district judge must certify these findings in writing
5. The court of appeals has discretion to permit the appeal within 10 days

**Timing:** 10 days from the district judge's certification to petition the court of appeals.

### Path 2: Cohen Collateral-Order Doctrine

> "Interlocious orders that are 'effectively final' — that decide the question, determine the right, and leave nothing for the court to do but execute the judgment — are appealable even though the case as a whole is not yet over." — Cohen v. Beneficial Industrial Loan Corp., 337 U.S. 541, 546 (1949)

**Requirements for collateral-order appeal:**
1. The order must "finally determine" the disputed question
2. The order must resolve an important issue completely separate from the merits
3. The order must be effectively unreviewable on appeal from a final judgment

### Path 3: Mandamus (28 U.S.C. § 1651)

> "The Supreme Court and all courts established by Act of Congress may issue writs in the exercise of jurisdiction attributable to their respective jurisdictions." — 28 U.S.C. § 1651 (All Writs Act)

**Requirements for mandamus:**
1. Clear and indisputable right to the writ
2. No adequate alternative remedy (e.g., waiting for final judgment)
3. Extraordinary circumstances

**Note:** Mandamus is rarely granted and is considered an "extraordinary remedy." It requires a higher showing than § 1292(b).
