# Curtiss-Wright Factor Analysis Block

**Authority:** Curtiss-Wright Corp. v. General Electric Co., 446 U.S. 1 (1980)
**Rule:** FRCP 54(b) — court must make express determination of "no just reason for delay" before certifying partial final judgment
**Binding on EDPA:** Yes — Supreme Court precedent + 3rd Circuit application

## Factor-by-Factor Analysis

### Factor 1: Does the claim involve a separate subject matter from the claims left open?

> "The determination of whether there is 'no just reason for delay' requires consideration of the relative completeness and consistency of the litigation, the importance of the questions decided, and the possibility of appellate review of a partial judgment." — Curtiss-Wright, 446 U.S. at 10

> "A determination that there is no just reason for delay is often appropriate when the claims disposed of are separate from the claims left open and there is a risk of inconsistent results." — See Berkeley Invitational Group, Ltd. v. Colkitt, 455 F.3d 195, 207 (3d Cir. 2006)

## Supporting Authority

- **Curtiss-Wright Corp. v. General Electric Co., 446 U.S. 1 (1980)** — Supreme Court establishes the five-factor test for FRCP 54(b) certification
- **Berkeley Invitational Group, Ltd. v. Colkitt, 455 F.3d 195 (3d Cir. 2006)** — Third Circuit requires "specific and reasoned" determination, not conclusory denial
- **FRCP 54(b)** — "When more than one claim for relief is presented in an action... the court may direct entry of a final judgment as to one or more but fewer than all of the claims only upon an express determination that there is no just reason for delay"
- **FRCP 58** — "Every judgment must be set forth on a separate document" — a bare order without findings is not a proper judgment under 54(b)

## Attack Vector

If the court denies 54(b) certification without making the required express determination and factor analysis:
1. The denial is procedurally defective under Curtiss-Wright
2. The denial forecloses meaningful appellate review
3. The party may pursue 28 U.S.C. § 1292(b) interlocutory appeal as an alternative path
4. The party may file mandamus to compel the district court to act on the 54(b) motion
