# FCRA Willful Liability Survives Pleadings

**15 U.S.C. § 1681n(a)** imposes liability on any person who "willfully fails to comply with any requirement imposed under this subchapter." The Supreme Court in *Safeco Ins. Co. v. Burr*, 551 U.S. 47 (2007), held that "willfulness" under the FCRA reaches reckless disregard — "an unjustifiably high risk of harm that is known or obvious" — not just knowing violations. *Safeco*, 551 U.S. at 71.

**Willfulness includes reckless disregard.** *Safeco*, 551 U.S. at 71; *McLaughlin v. Richland Shoe Co.*, 486 U.S. 128, 133 (1988) (willfulness in civil liability covers "knowing and reckless" violations under the traditional common-law understanding). Whether a defendant's failure to comply was willful (knowing or reckless) is a fact-intensive inquiry that cannot be resolved on the pleadings alone.

**The record contains genuine disputes of material fact on willfulness.** Defendants updated and removed tradeline data during litigation — an admission by conduct that prior reporting was inaccurate. This creates a genuine dispute as to whether defendants' noncompliance was willful (knowing or reckless) under the *Safeco* standard. Whether defendants knew or should have known of the risk of harm is a question of fact that requires further development.

**Judgment on the pleadings is improper because willfulness is a fact-intensive inquiry.** *Safeco*, 551 U.S. at 71; *Celotex Corp. v. Catrett*, 477 U.S. 317, 322 (1986). The moving party bears the initial burden of demonstrating the absence of a genuine dispute of material fact. *Celotex*, 477 U.S. at 323. Here, the record contains admissions by conduct (updated/removed data) that create a genuine dispute on willfulness. The motion must be denied.
