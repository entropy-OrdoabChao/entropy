## Temporary Restraining Order / Preliminary Injunction Under FRCP 65

### A. The Standard

A party may seek a temporary restraining order (TRO) or preliminary injunction before final judgment:

- **FRCP 65(b) — TRO**: A TRO may be issued without notice only if specific facts in an affidavit or verified complaint show that immediate and irreparable injury will result before the adverse party can be heard in opposition. The TRO must state the reasons for issuance, be set for hearing promptly, and expire within 14 days unless extended for good cause.

- **FRCP 65(a) — Preliminary Injunction**: A preliminary injunction may issue only after notice and hearing, on terms the court considers just. The court may consolidate the TRO hearing with the preliminary injunction hearing.

- **FRCP 65(c) — Security**: The court may require the party seeking an injunction to give security in an amount the court considers proper to pay the costs and damages sustained by any party found to have been wrongfully enjoined.

### B. Elements — Preliminary Injunction

To obtain a preliminary injunction, the movant must show:

1. **Irreparable injury**: The injury cannot be adequately remedied by money damages;
2. **Likelihood of success on the merits**: The movant has a reasonable probability of prevailing at trial;
3. **Balance of equities**: The threatened injury to the movant outweighs the harm the injunction would cause the opposing party; and
4. **Public interest**: The injunction would not adversely affect the public interest.

### C. Elements — TRO

For an ex parte TRO, the movant must additionally show:

1. That the movant has made a good-faith effort to provide notice, or why notice should not be required;
2. That the movant or counsel has made reasonable efforts to notify the adverse party; and
3. That the movant has made reasonable efforts to determine the identity of the adverse party's counsel of record.
