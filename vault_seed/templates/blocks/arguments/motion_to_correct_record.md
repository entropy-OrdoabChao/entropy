# Motion to Correct the Record — Argument Block

## A. The Court Has the Authority and Duty to Correct the Record Under FRAP 10(e)

Federal Rule of Appellate Procedure 10(e) provides: "On appeal the record should be corrected if there is any difference between what actually occurred and what the reporter's transcription shows." The rule is not limited to transcription errors — it extends to any material omission in the appellate record.

The court has inherent authority to correct clerical errors in the record. The Clerk of Court has a ministerial duty to maintain an accurate docket. When the record fails to reflect a document that was actually filed and delivered, the court must correct the record to reflect reality.

## B. The Record Is Missing the Appellant's Brief

Here, the record reflects that the appeal was dismissed for "failure to file brief." However, the appellant DID file a brief. The brief was delivered to the Clerk's Office on [DATE] via [METHOD], as evidenced by [EXHIBIT]. The Clerk's failure to reflect this filing is a material omission that must be corrected under FRAP 10(e).

## C. The Corrected Record Supports Reinstatement

Once the record is corrected to show that the brief was actually filed, the basis for the dismissal evaporates. The appeal should be reinstated because the appellant did not fail to prosecute — the Clerk's Office failed to record what was actually filed.

## D. Pro Se Liberal Construction Applies

As a pro se litigant, the appellant is entitled to liberal construction of filings under Haines v. Kerner, 404 U.S. 519 (1972). The court should construe the appellant's submissions liberally and correct the record to reflect the reality of what was filed.
