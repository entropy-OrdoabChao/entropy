## I. THE COURT HAS A DUTY TO STATE THE REASONS FOR ITS RULING

A court must decide on the merits of the arguments presented and articulate the basis for its
ruling sufficient to permit meaningful review. Under **FRCP 52(a)(1)**, in matters tried on facts the
court must find the facts specially and state its conclusions of law; reasoned decision-making is the
baseline of due process. A conclusory order that denies a motion without addressing the controlling
authority or the well-pleaded facts — which, for a *pro se* litigant, must be construed liberally
(*Haines v. Kerner*, 404 U.S. 519 (1972); *Erickson v. Pardus*, 551 U.S. 89 (2007)) — forecloses the
review to which Plaintiff is entitled.
