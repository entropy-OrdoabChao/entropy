# FCRA Negligent Liability Survives Pleadings

**15 U.S.C. § 1681o(a)** imposes liability on any person who is "negligent in failing to comply with any requirement imposed under this subchapter." Negligence is a fact-specific inquiry — whether the defendant "followed reasonable procedures to assure maximum possible accuracy." *15 U.S.C. § 1681e(b)*.

**The record contains genuine disputes of material fact on negligence.** Defendants updated and removed tradeline data during litigation — an admission by conduct that prior reporting was inaccurate. This creates a genuine dispute as to whether defendants followed "reasonable procedures to assure maximum possible accuracy" under § 1681e(b). Whether defendants' procedures were reasonable is a question of fact that requires further development.

**Judgment on the pleadings is improper because negligence is a fact-intensive inquiry.** *Celotex Corp. v. Catrett*, 477 U.S. 317, 322 (1986); *Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc.*, 477 U.S. 242, 247-48 (1986). The moving party bears the initial burden of demonstrating the absence of a genuine dispute of material fact. *Celotex*, 477 U.S. at 323. Here, the record contains admissions by conduct (updated/removed data) that create a genuine dispute on negligence. The motion must be denied.

**Furnisher duties under § 1681s-2(b) similarly survive pleadings.** The furnisher must investigate disputes, provide accurate information, and notify CRAs of corrections. Whether the furnisher fulfilled its reinvestigation obligations is fact-intensive — the record of updated bureau responses during litigation directly contradicts a "no genuine dispute" showing. *15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2(b)-(c)*.
