## Counsel's Failure to Disclose Was a Candor Violation

Pennsylvania Rule of Professional Conduct 3.3 -- and its federal
counterpart, Model Rule 3.3 -- require that a lawyer "**not knowingly**"
fail to disclose to a tribunal "**legal authority in the controlling
jurisdiction known to the lawyer to be directly adverse to the position
of the client and not disclosed by opposing counsel.**"

The plain text of Rule 3.3 reaches more than just on-point precedent
that cuts against the lawyer's argument. It reaches the *status* of the
authorities a lawyer cites. When counsel cites an order without
disclosing that the order is the subject of a pending Petition for Writ
of Mandamus -- particularly in proceedings between the same parties or
parties in privity -- the failure is not merely strategic incompleteness.
It deprives the tribunal of material information about the authority's
weight and validity.

The conduct here also implicates Pa.R.C.P. 1023.1 and FRCP 11. By
signing and filing papers that present a contested order as settled,
counsel certified -- to the best of their knowledge after reasonable
inquiry -- that the legal contentions therein were "warranted by existing
law." A contention warranted only by an order the validity of which is
actively under appellate challenge does not satisfy that certification.

Plaintiff requests that this Court take the candor failure into
account when weighing Defendants' submissions, and respectfully
submits that the appropriate remedies include: (1) striking the
filings that relied on the contested order; (2) sanctions under
Pa.R.C.P. 1023.4 / FRCP 11(c) sufficient to deter recurrence; and
(3) referral to disciplinary counsel as the Court deems appropriate.
