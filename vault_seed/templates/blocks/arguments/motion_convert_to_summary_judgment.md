# Motion Converts to Summary Judgment When Extrinsic Materials Considered

**When a court considers extrinsic materials in response to a motion for judgment on the pleadings, the motion must be treated as one for summary judgment under Fed. R. Civ. P. 56.** Fed. R. Civ. P. 12(c); Fed. R. Civ. P. 56(c). "If, on a motion under Rule 12(b)(6) or Rule 12(c), matters outside the pleadings are presented to and not excluded by the court, the motion must be treated as one for summary judgment under Rule 56." Fed. R. Civ. P. 12(c).

**The court must give all parties a reasonable opportunity to present all material pertinent to the motion.** Fed. R. Civ. P. 56(c). This includes the right to file affidavits, discovery materials, and other evidence. *Celotex Corp. v. Catrett*, 477 U.S. 317, 324 (1986).

**In this case, TransUnion has presented extrinsic materials (the record of updated/removed data, 1099-C forms, credit report comparisons) that are NOT matters of public record.** These materials create genuine disputes of material fact that cannot be resolved on the pleadings alone. The court must either:
1. Exclude the extrinsic materials and decide the motion on the pleadings; or
2. Convert the motion to summary judgment and allow the parties to present all material pertinent to the motion.

**If the court converts the motion to summary judgment, the record supports summary judgment for Plaintiff.** The record contains admissions by conduct (updated/removed data) that create genuine disputes on willfulness, negligence, and UTPCPL conduct. The moving party bears the initial burden of demonstrating the absence of a genuine dispute of material fact. *Celotex*, 477 U.S. at 323. Here, the moving party cannot meet that burden because the record contains admissions by conduct that create genuine disputes. The motion must be denied.
