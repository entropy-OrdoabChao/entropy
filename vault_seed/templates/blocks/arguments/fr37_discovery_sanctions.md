## Discovery Sanctions Under FRCP 37

### A. The Standard — FRCP 37

Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 37 provides the mechanisms and sanctions for discovery abuses:

- **FRCP 37(a)(5)** — When a motion to compel discovery is denied, the court may award the movant reasonable expenses, including attorney's fees, if the losing party acted without substantial justification or if other circumstances make an award of expenses appropriate.

- **FRCP 37(b)(2)** — If a party fails to comply with a discovery order, the court may impose sanctions including: (A) treating the failure as contempt of court; (B) ordering that designated facts be taken as established; (C) prohibiting the disobedient party from supporting or opposing designated claims or defenses; (D) striking pleadings; (E) staying proceedings; (F) dismissing the action; or (G) rendering a default judgment.

- **FRCP 37(c)(2)** — If a party fails to admit the genuineness of any document or the truth of any matter as requested under FRCP 36, and if the party requesting admission thereafter proves the genuineness or truth, the court may order the party who failed to admit to pay the reasonable expenses incurred in making that proof, including reasonable attorney's fees.

- **FRCP 37(d)** — If a party fails to respond to a request for discovery, the court may impose sanctions including dismissal of the action or entry of a default judgment.

- **FRCP 37(e)** — If electronically stored information that should have been preserved is lost because a party failed to take reasonable steps to preserve it, and it cannot be restored or replaced, the court may order measures no greater than necessary to cure the prejudice or, if warranted, presume the lost information was unfavorable or dismiss the action.

### B. Application — Why Discovery Sanctions Are Warranted

The defects identified above — particularly the construction defects and discovery abuses — demonstrate that the opposing party has failed to comply with the discovery framework and has submitted filings that contain factual and legal contentions not warranted by the record. The failure to produce responsive discovery, the citation of non-binding authority as controlling, and the misapplication of construction rules constitute the kind of discovery abuse that triggers FRCP 37(b)(2) sanctions.

Under FRCP 37(b)(2)(B), the court may order that designated facts be taken as established — effectively treating the opposing party's claims as admitted for failure to comply with discovery orders. Under FRCP 37(b)(2)(E), the court may stay proceedings against the disobedient party. Under FRCP 37(b)(2)(F), the court may dismiss the action.
