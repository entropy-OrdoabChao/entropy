## Rule 11 Sanctions for Defective Legal Contentions

### A. The Substantive Standard — FRCP 11(b)

Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 11(b) requires that every paper, motion, and other writing submitted to the court be signed by a party or counsel and thereby constitutes a certification to the court that:

1. **The paper has not been presented for any improper purpose** — such as to harass, cause unnecessary delay, or needlessly increase the cost of litigation;

2. **The claims and defenses are warranted by existing law or by a non-frivolous argument for extending, modifying, or reversing existing law** — and are not frivolous;

3. **The factual contentions have evidentiary support or, if specifically so identified, will likely have evidentiary support after a reasonable opportunity for further investigation or discovery**; and

4. **The denials of factual contentions are warranted on the evidence or, if specifically so identified, on a reasonable inquiry of the matter.**

### B. The Safe Harbor — FRCP 11(c)(2)

Before filing a motion for Rule 11 sanctions, the movant must serve the motion on the opposing party and provide a **21-day waiting period** during which the opposing party may withdraw or correct the challenged filing. This is the "safe harbor" provision — it gives the offending party a chance to correct the problem before sanctions are imposed.

### C. The Sanctions — FRCP 11(c)(1) and (c)(4)

If the court determines that Rule 11(b) has been violated, the court may impose an **appropriate sanction** on any attorney, law firm, or party that violated the rule or is responsible for the violation. Absent exceptional circumstances, a law firm must be held jointly responsible for a violation committed by its partner, associate, or employee.

Per FRCP 11(c)(4), sanctions may be nonmonetary directives (e.g., admonition, reprimand, censure, educational seminars), an order to pay a penalty into court, or an order directing payment of reasonable attorney's fees and expenses to the movant.

### D. Application — Why Sanctions Are Warranted

The defects identified above — non-binding authority cited as controlling, failure to disclose the contested status of cited orders, procedural non-conformities, and misapplication of construction rules — are not technical missteps. They are choices, made and re-made across multiple filings, that shift to Plaintiff (and to this Court) the work of identifying infirmities that should never have entered the record.

Under FRCP 11(b)(2) and (b)(3), the defective legal contentions are not warranted by existing law and lack evidentiary support. Under FRCP 11(b)(1), they have been presented for the improper purpose of needlessly increasing the cost of litigation through the burden of correcting the record.

### E. Requested Relief

Plaintiff respectfully requests the following sanctions:

1. An order requiring Defendants to re-file the contested papers with corrected authority and full disclosure of pending challenges;

2. Plaintiff's reasonable costs and expenses incurred in identifying and briefing the defects; and

3. Such further relief as the Court deems just under FRCP 11(c) or Pa.R.C.P. 1023.4.
