## Rule 11 Sanctions for Defective Legal Contentions

### A. The Standard — FRCP 11(b)

Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 11(b) requires that every pleading, motion, and other paper be certified as:

- **(b)(1)** Not presented for any improper purpose — harassment, delay, or needless increase in cost
- **(b)(2)** Not frivolous — not legally tenable under existing law or supported by a non-frivolous argument for extension/modification/reversal
- **(b)(3)** Not without factual basis — not supported by evidence for factual contentions
- **(b)(4)** Not frivolous — not legally tenable under existing law for factual contentions

**FRCP 11(c)** provides that if a violation is found, the court must impose an appropriate sanction — which may include:
- An order directing payment to the court of a penalty
- An order directing payment to the prevailing party of part or all of the reasonable attorney's fees and expenses
- Other sanctions the court deems appropriate

### B. Sanctions Are Warranted Here

The filings by opposing counsel contain multiple violations of FRCP 11(b):

1. **Non-binding authority cited as controlling** — Citing authority from other circuits (e.g., 6th Cir., 8th Cir., D.N.J.) as if it binds this court in violation of FRCP 11(b)(2). This is a legal contention that has no basis in existing law and is not supported by any non-frivolous argument for extension or modification.

2. **Citation of non-precedential dispositions as binding authority** — Relying on non-precedential per curiam dispositions as if they establish binding law in violation of FRCP 11(b)(2). The Third Circuit's own IOP 5.7 forecloses such reliance.

3. **Factual findings contradicted by the docket record** — Making factual findings that are directly contradicted by the court's own docket in violation of FRCP 11(b)(4).

**IF** opposing counsel cited non-binding authority from other circuits as if it controls, AND the court's own docket contradicts the factual findings relied upon, THEN the filings violate FRCP 11(b) and sanctions under FRCP 11(c) are warranted.

### C. Safe Harbor Compliance — FRCP 11(c)(2)

Plaintiff acknowledges that FRCP 11(c)(2) requires a 21-day safe harbor period before sanctions can be imposed. Plaintiff requests that this motion be treated as the required safe harbor notice. If opposing counsel does not withdraw or correct the challenged filings within 21 days of service of this motion, sanctions should be imposed.
