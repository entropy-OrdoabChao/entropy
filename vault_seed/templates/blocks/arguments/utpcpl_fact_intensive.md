# UTPCPL Claims Are Inherently Fact-Intensive

**73 Pa. Stat. Ann. § 201-2(4)** prohibits "unfair or deceptive acts or practices." The Pennsylvania Supreme Court has held that UTPCPL claims are inherently fact-intensive and require inquiry into the defendant's conduct — precisely what pleadings cannot resolve. *Pittsburgh v. Barasch*, 541 A.2d 508 (Pa. 1988); *Berge v. Commonwealth*, 447 A.2d 116 (Pa. 1982).

UTPCPL claims require specific allegations of misrepresentation, concealment, or omission causing unjust disadvantage or substantial injury. *73 Pa. Stat. Ann. § 201-2(4)*. These claims demand inquiry into the defendant's conduct — the accuracy of its statements, the completeness of its disclosures, and the fairness of its practices. Such inquiries are fact-intensive and cannot be resolved on the pleadings alone.

**Treble damages and attorney fees are available under § 201-9.2.** *73 Pa. Stat. Ann. § 201-9.2*. The availability of treble damages requires a finding of willfulness or reckless disregard — a factual inquiry that cannot be made on the pleadings.

**Because UTPCPL claims are fact-intensive, judgment on the pleadings is improper.** *Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc.*, 477 U.S. 242, 247-48 (1986); *Celotex Corp. v. Catrett*, 477 U.S. 317, 322 (1986). The moving party bears the initial burden of demonstrating the absence of a genuine dispute of material fact. *Celotex*, 477 U.S. at 323. Here, the record contains admissions by conduct (updated/removed data, 1099-C forms, credit report comparisons) that create genuine disputes on UTPCPL conduct. The motion must be denied.
