## VIII. ACCOUNTABILITY: WHO IS STATUTORILY EMPOWERED TO COMPEL COMPLIANCE

### A. The Rule

For every defendant, the statute or regulation identifies who is empowered to COMPEL compliance and where to route the complaint:

- **Regulator** — The agency that enforces the statute (e.g., CFPB for FCRA, HUD for RESPA)
- **OIG** — The Inspector General of the relevant agency (e.g., HUD OIG, CFPB OIG)
- **Special Counsel** — Under 5 U.S.C. § 2302(b)(8) and 552(a)(4)(F), for federal agencies
- **Judicial Conduct Board (JCB)** — For judicial misconduct (28 U.S.C. § 351)
- **28 U.S.C. § 455** — Judicial disqualification for bias or conflict
- **ODC / Rule 11** — For opposing counsel's conduct
- **28 U.S.C. § 1927** — Counsel liability for excessive costs

### C. No Party Escapes Accountability

No party escapes accountability. The judge is accountable under 28 U.S.C. § 351 and 28 U.S.C. § 455. Opposing counsel is accountable under Rule 11 and 28 U.S.C. § 1927. Each defendant is accountable under the statute that imposes the duty.
