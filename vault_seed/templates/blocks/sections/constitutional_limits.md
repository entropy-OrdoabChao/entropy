## VI. CONSTITUTIONAL LIMITS

### A. Taxing Clause

The Taxing Clause (U.S. Const., Art. I, § 8, cl. 1) limits the power to impose taxes to those for a public purpose. **Gould v. Gould, 245 U.S. 151 (1917):** Doubt is construed AGAINST the government in favor of the citizen. This principle extends beyond taxing provisions — when a statute defines a term, the definition controls.

### B. Spending Clause

The Spending Clause (U.S. Const., Art. I, § 8, cl. 1) authorizes Congress to spend for the general welfare. Conditions on the receipt of federal funds must be unambiguous and voluntary. **South Dakota v. Dole, 483 U.S. 203 (1987).**

### C. Apportionment Clause

The Apportionment Clause (U.S. Const., Art. I, § 9, cl. 4) requires direct taxes to be apportioned among the states by population. **Brushaber v. Union Pacific R.R. Co., 240 U.S. 1 (1916).**

### D. Due Process Notice

The Due Process Clause (U.S. Const., amend. V / XIV) requires notice reasonably calculated to inform interested parties of the pendency of the action. **Mullane v. Central Hanover Bank & Trust Co., 339 U.S. 306 (1950).**

### E. Art. III Standing as Applied

The standing inquiry asks: (1) injury in fact — concrete, particularized, actual or imminent; (2) causation — fairly traceable to the challenged conduct; (3) redressability — likely to be redressed by a favorable judicial decision. **Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555 (1992); Spokeo, Inc. v. Robins, 578 U.S. 330 (2016); TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez, 594 U.S. 413 (2021).**

For this case: The plaintiff's injury must be concrete and particularized — not abstract, speculative, or hypothetical. The injury must be fairly traceable to the defendant's conduct and likely to be redressed by a favorable decision. **Spokeo, 578 U.S. at 340.**
