## V. DEFINITIONS SCHEDULE AND CANONS OF CONSTRUCTION — Y/N

A judicial order must apply the correct definitions of the law. When a Court substitutes its own definition for the one prescribed by statute, regulation, or binding precedent, the order is not merely wrong — it is a breach of the judge's duty to apply the law as written.

### A. Definitions Schedule

Under the **plain meaning rule** (the first canon of statutory construction), courts must give effect to the ordinary meaning of statutory text. **Chevron U.S.A., Inc. v. NRDC, 467 U.S. 837, 842-843 (1984)**: "If the intent of Congress is clear, that is the end of the matter; for the court, as well as the agency, must give effect to the unambiguously expressed intent of Congress."

### B. Canons of Construction — Operative-Text Discipline

**Canon 0: Operative Text** — BEFORE any construction rule applies, QUOTE the operative word or phrase from the statute/regulation. The construction rule the statute itself supplies governs; no canon may be applied without first verifying the operative text. The operative word is the word that controls the class — its definition determines the scope.

**Canon 1a: MEANS = Closed Frame** — If a statute defines a term with "means," the defined term IS EXHAUSTIVE. Nothing outside the definition is within its scope. No expansion by implication, no broadening by context, no addition of qualifiers not in the text. The enumeration defines the class.

**Canon 1b: INCLUDES ≠ Automatically Expansive** — If a statute defines a term with "includes," the defined term is NOT automatically expansive. The statute supplies its own construction rule:
  - **IF the statute carries an explicit expansion clause** (e.g., 26 U.S.C. § 7701(c) — applies only to definitions CONTAINED IN THIS TITLE), QUOTE the clause and note its bounded scope: the expansion applies only to definitions contained within the same title, not to the defined term itself.
  - **IF the statute has NO expansion clause**, the default canons govern: EJUSDEM GENERIS (general words following an enumeration are limited to the same class) and EXPRESSIO UNIUS (expression of one thing excludes others) — the ENUMERATION DEFINES THE CLASS.
  - Never wire a blanket includes=expansive rule. The statute dictates the construction.

**Canon 1c: DEFINED-TERM CHAIN TRACING** — When a definition hangs on another defined term, trace the chain link by link. QUOTE each link. If a mid-chain swap of the defined term for its common-word homonym occurs, flag it as a citable defect. The chain must stay inside the SAME statute unless a cross-reference imports it.

**Canon 1d: NO-WORD-ADDED-OR-SUBTRACTED** — Quote the opposing rendering of a definition next to the verbatim text. Every silently inserted word (e.g., "only") or deleted limiter is a facial departure from the statutory text. This cuts BOTH ways: an opponent silently EXPANDING an enumerated definition without an expansion clause is the same defect as silently SHRINKING one. Candor hooks Pa.R.P.C. 3.3 / Rule 11(b)(2).

**Canon 2: Expressio Unius Est Exclusio Alterius** — The expression of one thing is the exclusion of another. If the Court's Order includes one category and excludes another similarly situated category without explanation, the exclusion is a defect.

**Canon 3: Ejusdem Generis** — Where general words follow specific words, the general words are limited to things of the same kind as the specific words. If the Court's Order uses a general term to sweep in matters not of the same kind as the specific terms, the Order is defective.

**Canon 4: Noscitur a Sociis** — A word is known by the company it keeps. If the Court's Order applies a term in a way inconsistent with its surrounding context, the Order is defective.

**Canon 5: Contra Proferentem** — Ambiguity in a rule or order is construed against the drafter. If the Court's Order is ambiguous and the ambiguity is resolved against the moving party, the ambiguity must be resolved in favor of the party against whom the Order is directed.

**Canon 6: Harmonization** — A Court must read its Order so that all parts are harmonious and none is rendered surplusage. If the Court's Order contains irreconcilable directives, the Order is defective.

**Canon 7: Nemo Judex in Causa Sua** — No one should be a judge in his own cause. If the Court's Order benefits the Court or its officers, the Order is defective.
