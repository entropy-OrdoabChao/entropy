## III. FRCP 54(b) CERTIFICATION QUESTIONNAIRE — Y/N

**FRCP 54(b)** provides: "When more than one claim for relief is presented in an action — whether as a claim, counterclaim, crossclaim, or third-party claim — or when multiple parties are involved, the court may direct the entry of a final judgment as to one or more but fewer than all of the claims or parties only if the court expressly determines that there is no just reason for delay."

The Supreme Court in **Curtiss-Wright Corp. v. General Electric Co., 446 U.S. 1 (1980)** established that a district court must make an express determination on the record before directing entry of a partial final judgment under Rule 54(b). The court must consider the factors identified in **Sussex Drug Products Corp. v. Kanasco, Ltd., 920 F.2d 1150 (3d Cir. 1990)**.

### A. The Express Determination Requirement

> **Y/N:** Did the Court make the express determination required by FRCP 54(b) before directing entry of a final judgment?

Under **Curtiss-Wright**, 446 U.S. at 4, "Rule 54(b) requires that the district court make an express determination that there is no just reason for delay and direct the clerk to enter judgment." This determination must be express — not implied, not assumed, not inferred from silence.

> **IF** the Court directed the clerk to enter judgment without making the express determination required by FRCP 54(b), AND the Court's oath demands that the judge act within the bounds of the law, THEN the Court's action constitutes a breach of the oath obligation and is subject to correction.

### B. The "No Just Reason for Delay" Factors

Under **Curtiss-Wright** and **Sussex Drug**, the court must consider the following factors:

1. **The relationship between the adjudicated and unadjudicated claims.**
2. **Whether the adjudicated claim might be subject to different standards of decision if reviewed on a different factual record.**
3. **Whether the claim for relief has been subject to multiple hearings or extensive briefing.**
4. **Whether the claim for relief is contingent.**
5. **Whether a delay might create a risk of inequitable administration of justice.**

> **IF** the Court directed entry of judgment without considering the Curtiss-Wright factors, AND the Court's oath demands that the judge act within the bounds of the law, THEN the Court's action constitutes a breach of the oath obligation and is subject to correction.

### C. The Right to Appeal

Under **28 U.S.C. § 1291**, the courts of appeals have jurisdiction over "all final decisions of the district courts." Without a proper FRCP 54(b) certification, a partial judgment is not "final" and cannot be appealed.

> **IF** the Court directed entry of judgment that is not final under FRCP 54(b), AND the Court's oath demands that the judge act within the bounds of the law, THEN the Court's action constitutes a breach of the oath obligation and is subject to correction.
