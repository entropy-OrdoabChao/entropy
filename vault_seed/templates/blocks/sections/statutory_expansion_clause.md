## IX. STATUTORY EXPANSION CLAUSE CHECK

### A. The Rule

**IF** a statute defines a term with "includes" and carries an explicit expansion clause,
**AND** the expansion clause limits the scope of the expansion (e.g., "applies only to definitions contained in this title"),
**THEN** the expansion is BOUNDED by the clause.

**IF** the statute has NO expansion clause,
**AND** the opponent treats "includes" as automatically expansive,
**THEN** the opponent is applying a construction rule the statute does not supply.

The default canons govern: **EJUSDEM GENERIS** (general words following an enumeration are limited to the same class) and **EXPRESSIO UNIUS** (expression of one thing excludes others) — the ENUMERATION DEFINES THE CLASS.

### C. Authority: The Statute Supplies Its Own Construction Rule

> **26 U.S.C. § 7701(c):** "Unless the context otherwise requires, when used in this title, words importing the singular include the plural." — The expansion is limited to the title; it does not expand the defined term itself.

> **Gould v. Gould, 245 U.S. 151, 153 (1917):** "Doubt is construed against the government, in favor of the citizen." — The opponent's construction must be against them.
