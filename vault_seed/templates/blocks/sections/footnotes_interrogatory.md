## IV. FOOTNOTES INTERROGATORY — TRACING THE RECORD

A judge's footnotes are not mere afterthoughts — they are part of the judicial order and carry the weight of the Court's reasoning. **FRCP 52(a)** requires findings of fact and conclusions of law for each material issue — and footnotes are where the Court often places its most significant reasoning.

> **Y/N:** Did the Court's footnotes at Dkt {{latest_order.dkt_no}} contain findings of fact sufficient to satisfy FRCP 52(a)?

Under **FRCP 52(a)**, when the Court decides a motion on the basis of factual findings — even if the motion is governed by summary judgment standards — the Court must state its findings of fact and conclusions of law. **Fed. R. Civ. P. 52(a)(1)**: "In granting or denying interlocutory injunctions, the court must state its findings of fact and conclusions of law."

Under **Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242 (1986)**, the Court must make findings of fact that are "expressly found to be necessary" under FRCP 52(a).

> **IF** the Court's footnotes contain findings of fact without the express factual findings required by FRCP 52(a), AND the Court's oath demands that the judge act within the bounds of the law, THEN the Court's action constitutes a breach of the oath obligation and is subject to correction.

### A. Footnote Citations — Are They to the Record?

> **Y/N:** Do the Court's footnotes cite to the actual record — or to authority not in the record?

When a Court cites to a document not in the record, the citation is not binding precedent — it is an assertion without a record basis. The opposing party has no opportunity to test the accuracy or completeness of the citation.

Under **Fed. R. Evid. 201(b)(2)**, the Court may take judicial notice of facts "not subject to reasonable dispute" because they "can be accurately and readily determined from sources whose accuracy cannot reasonably be questioned." But the Court cannot take judicial notice of a party's submission as if it were a source of judicial notice — the party's own submission is not a judicial-notice source.

> **IF** the Court's footnotes cite to a document not in the record, AND the Court's oath demands that the judge act within the bounds of the law, THEN the Court's action constitutes a breach of the oath obligation and is subject to correction.

### B. Footnote Reasoning — Is It Consistent with the Order?

> **Y/N:** Is the Court's footnoted reasoning consistent with the operative holding of the Order?

When a Court's footnotes contradict the operative holding of the Order — for example, by acknowledging a fact that undermines the Order's reasoning — the inconsistency itself is a defect that demands resolution. The Court cannot have it both ways: it cannot find one thing in its footnotes and rule another in its holding.

> **IF** the Court's footnotes contradict the operative holding of the Order, AND the Court's oath demands that the judge administer justice, THEN the inconsistency constitutes a breach of the oath obligation and is subject to correction.
