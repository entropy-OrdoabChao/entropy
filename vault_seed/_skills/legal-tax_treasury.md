### Research — When is cancellation of debt treated as income, and what form reports it? Cite the Code.
CANCELLATION OF DEBT (COD) AS INCOME — GROUNDED ANSWER

1. THE RULE: COD is gross income by statutory default

26 U.S.C. § 61(a)(11): "gross income means all income from whatever source
derived, including... (11) Income from discharge of indebtedness."
[official: 26 U.S.C. § 61(a)(11), verified against LII text]

Note on numbering: this was § 61(a)(12) before the TCJA; Pub. L. 115-97
§ 11051(b)(1)(A) (2017) struck the alimony paragraph and redesignated (12)
as (11). Older authorities citing 61(a)(12) refer to the same provision.
[official: Pub. L. 115-97, 131 Stat. 2089]

So the timing answer: debt cancellation is treated as income IN THE YEAR OF
DISCHARGE, as ordinary income, UNLESS a statutory exclusion applies.

2. THE EXCLUSIONS: 26 U.S.C. § 108

§ 108(a)(1) excludes COD income from gross income when the discharge occurs:
  (A) in a title 11 (bankruptcy) case;
  (B) while the taxpayer is insolvent (capped at the amount of insolvency);
  (C) qualified farm indebtedness;
  (D) qualified real property business indebtedness (non-C-corp taxpayers);
  (E) qualified principal residence indebtedness (time-limited — the corpus
      copy of the IRS guidance reflects "discharged before 2025, or subject
      to a written arrangement entered before January 1, 2026").
[corpus: Treas. Reg. §§ 1.108-5, 1.108-7, 1.108-9 chunks confirming
 108(a)(1)(A)-(B) bankruptcy/insolvency exclusions and attribute reduction;
 IRS pub text on the five exclusions]

The exclusions are not free: § 108(b) requires reduction of tax attributes
(NOLs, credits, basis) by the excluded amount — the corpus regs (Treas. Reg.
§ 1.108-7) show the proportional attribute-reduction mechanics. [corpus]
Excluded amounts are claimed on Form 982 (Reduction of Tax Attributes Due to
Discharge of Indebtedness). [corpus: Treas. Reg. § 1.108-5(b) — "election is
to be made on a completed Form 982"]

3. THE REPORTING FORM: 1099-C, under 26 U.S.C. § 6050P

26 U.S.C. § 6050P(a): any "applicable entity" that discharges indebtedness
of any person "shall make a return" showing the debtor's name/TIN, the date
of discharge, and the amount discharged. § 6050P(b): no return required for
discharges under $600. [official: 26 U.S.C. § 6050P(a)-(b), verified]

Who must file — § 6050P(c): federal executive/judicial/legislative agencies;
banks, thrifts, credit unions; FDIC/NCUA and federal agencies; their
supervised subsidiaries; and "any organization a significant trade or
business of which is the lending of money." [official: § 6050P(c)(1)-(2)]

The prescribed return is Form 1099-C, Cancellation of Debt, per Treas. Reg.
§ 1.6050P-1 ("Information reporting for discharges of indebtedness by
certain entities"), which also defines the eight "identifiable events" fixing
the date of discharge, and excepts certain bankruptcy discharges and interest
from the reporting requirement. [corpus: § 1.6050P-0/-1 table of contents
chunk; IRM chunk: "If a debt is terminated and is $600 or more, the amount
is reported on Form 1099-C... interest portion... reported separately in
box 3"]

Where it lands on the debtor's return: nonbusiness canceled debt goes on
Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 8 as other income; business debt goes on the
relevant business schedule (e.g., Schedule F for farm debt). [corpus: IRS
pub chunk]

4. KEY DISTINCTION — the 1099-C does not itself create tax liability

The reporting duty (§ 6050P) and the income inclusion (§§ 61/108) are
separate statutes with different tests. A creditor can be required to file a
1099-C on an "identifiable event" (e.g., expiration of its non-payment
testing period under Reg. § 1.6050P-1(b)(2)) even where the debtor excludes
the amount under § 108 — the regulation's own table of contents includes
"(a)(3) Amounts not includible in income." [corpus] So receipt of a 1099-C
is evidence of a reporting event, not a conclusive determination that the
amount is taxable income.
