### Audit — An opposing complaint in PA Common Pleas has no verification page. What rule is violated and what is the remedy? Brief, cite the rule.

Rule violated
- Pa.R.C.P. 1024(a) [official: 231 Pa. Code Rule 1024]: "Every pleading containing an averment of fact not appearing of record in the action or containing a denial of fact shall state that the averment or denial is true upon the signer's personal knowledge or information and belief and shall be verified." A complaint averring facts with no verification page violates this rule.
- Rule 1024(c) governs who must sign (a party; or a person with sufficient knowledge/authorization if no party is available, with an explanation of why the party didn't sign).

Remedy
- Preliminary objection under Pa.R.C.P. 1028(a)(2) [official: 231 Pa. Code Rule 1028(a)(2)] — "failure of a pleading to conform to law or rule of court" — typically framed as a motion to strike the complaint.
- Practical caveat (mark it, don't oversell it): Pennsylvania courts treat a missing/defective verification as a curable, amendable defect rather than grounds for outright dismissal in most cases — the usual outcome is leave to file a proper verification, and courts have said the defect can be waived if not raised promptly. So the PO forces cure and puts the signer on the hook for the facts; it rarely kills the case by itself. (This characterization is from general PA practice; the specific case law is NOT IN CORPUS on this pass — if you want it cited in a filing, I'll pull and verify actual authority via CourtListener before drafting.)

Why it still matters: verification is what exposes the pleading's averments to 18 Pa.C.S. § 4904 (unsworn falsification) [official cite — verify text before filing]. An unverified complaint is fact-averment without accountability — worth forcing the cure on the record.

---

I. THE COMPLAINT MUST BE STRICKEN BECAUSE IT CONTAINS AVERMENTS OF FACT NOT OF RECORD YET IS WHOLLY UNVERIFIED, IN VIOLATION OF Pa.R.Civ.P. 1024(a)

Rule 1024(a) commands that "[e]very pleading containing an averment of fact not appearing of record in the action . . . shall state that the averment or denial is true upon the signer's personal knowledge or information and belief and shall be verified," and Rule 1024(c) requires that the verification be made by a party — or, if by a non-party, that it set forth the source of the signer's information and the reason a party did not verify. Pa.R.Civ.P. 1024(a), (c). The Complaint pleads facts outside the record but carries no verification satisfying Rule 1024 in any form, a facial defect apparent from the pleading itself. Because a pleading that omits the mandatory verification fails to conform to a rule of court, this defect is properly raised by preliminary objection under Pa.R.Civ.P. 1028(a)(2), and the Complaint should be stricken, with any repleading subject to full compliance with Rule 1024.
