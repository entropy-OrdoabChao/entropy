# Getting your state's law into your desk

Federal law is already in your desk. Your state's law comes from your state's own official websites, and the desk downloads it for you when you pick your state. For some states, part of that can't be fetched automatically, because the state's site blocks programs or only shows the text inside a viewer. When that happens the desk asks you to get that piece yourself. This page explains how. It takes a few minutes per piece.

## What a "state pack" is

Fifteen pieces, always the same fifteen, for every state:

1. State constitution
2. Statutes (the state code)
3. Regulations (the administrative code)
4. State register or bulletin
5. Rules of civil procedure
6. Rules of appellate procedure
7. Court structure (which court your case starts in and where it climbs)
8. E-filing system
9. Consumer protection statutes
10. Financial and insurance regulators
11. Who takes complaints about judges
12. Who takes complaints about lawyers
13. Court opinions
14. Filing fees and the fee-waiver rule
15. Business registry (to find a company's exact name and registered agent)

Under **Settings › Courts and state**, pick your state. You will see one of:

- **Verified · loaded** — nothing to do.
- **Downloading** — the desk is fetching the pieces; you can keep working.
- **Some pieces need you** — a short list of what the desk could not fetch, with a button for each.

## Why the desk can't always fetch it

Three reasons, and the desk tells you which one applies:

- **The site blocks programs.** Many court and agency sites only answer a real browser. You open the page; it works for you.
- **The text lives inside a viewer.** Some states publish their code or rules through a contracted publisher's website (Westlaw or Lexis "public access"). It's still official, but the text is inside an app, so you save the sections you need.
- **The page is an app, not a document.** Business registries and some rules sites load in a script. The desk's guided browser handles these when you need them; nothing to download.

## How to get a missing piece

Every missing piece has the same three-step button in the desk: **Open the official page → Save it to my pack → Checked**.

1. **Open the official page.** The desk opens the exact official link in its own browser window. If the site asks you to prove you're human, do it. If it asks for a login (rare), use yours. The desk never sees your password.
2. **Save it to my pack.** When the page you need is on screen, press *Save this page to my pack*. The desk captures the page from your browser, or the PDF if you downloaded one. For a long code, use the site's "download" or "print to PDF" and drop the file on the desk instead. Many at once is fine.
3. **Checked.** The desk runs its check on what you saved: is it from the official site, is it the law as written (not an article about it), do its terms resolve, does it contradict anything already loaded. If it passes, the piece turns green and is now part of your library, citable. If it fails, the desk says why (for example, "this is a summary page, not the rule text; try the PDF link").

Tips:

- **Court rules on a publisher's site:** look for "Rules of Civil Procedure" in the left menu, open the rule set, and use the site's print or download-PDF button for the whole set. One file is enough.
- **Statutes on a publisher's site:** you don't need the whole code on day one. Save the title or chapter your case uses (the desk names it, for example "Title 15, Consumer Protection"). You can add more later the same way.
- **Regulations and the register:** the desk names the parts it needs for your case; save those.
- **Fee waiver:** save the rule and the form (usually a PDF called "Application to Proceed Without Payment of Fees" or "In Forma Pauperis").
- **Court opinions:** you don't download these. The desk looks them up when it needs them, from the court's site and from CourtListener.

## What never happens

- Nothing is added to your library unless it passes the check. A blog post, a summary, or a page from a non-official site is rejected with the reason shown.
- Nothing is sent anywhere. The pages you save stay on your computer.
- The desk never fills forms, pays fees or presses Submit on a state site. That's always your hand.

## If you get stuck

Under the missing piece there is *Tell us*. It sends us only the state and the piece name, nothing from your case, so we can fix the map for everyone.
