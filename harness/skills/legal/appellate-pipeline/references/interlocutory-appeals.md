# Interlocutory Appealability Barriers & Strategic Surrender

## Binding Precedent: District Court May Require Amendment or Dismissal with Prejudice

### *In re Westinghouse Secs. Litig.*, 90 F.3d 696, 704 (3d Cir. 1996)

**Holding:** District court may dismiss a plaintiff's claims with prejudice if:
1. District court gives plaintiff time to amend
2. Plaintiff refuses to amend
3. District court warns that failure to amend will result in dismissal with prejudice

**Appellate Effect:** Creates appealable final judgment; appellate court may review underlying legal error

### *Pruden v. SCI Camp Hill*, 252 F. App'x 436, 438 (3d Cir. 2007) (per curiam)

**Holding:** Applies *Westinghouse* rule in pro-se context. Pro-se litigants held to same standard.

**Key Language:** "The district court acted within its discretion in granting the appellees' motion to dismiss with prejudice when the pro se plaintiff failed to amend his complaint after being advised he could do so."

## Strategic Surrender Analysis

### When This Applies
- District court denies Motion to Reconsider/Vacate
- District court grants leave to amend by specific deadline
- Claims dismissed **without prejudice** (not final)
- Rule 54(b) certification unavailable (claims proceeding against other defendants)
- No §1292(b) certification for interlocutory appeal

### Strategic Decision Matrix

| Scenario | Recommended Action | Appealability |
|----------|-------------------|---------------|
| **Underlying error is clear, legal error is reversible** | Let deadline expire → dismissal with prejudice | **Appealable immediately** |
| **Underlying error is factual/discretionary** | Amend to preserve claims | No appeal until final judgment |
| **Multiple claims, some dismissed with prejudice** | Amend only claims dismissed without prejudice | Appeal only on those dismissed with prejudice |

### Risks of Strategic Surrender

1. **Procedural Default:** Appellate court may affirm based on refusal to amend without reaching merits (*Pruden*)
2. **Mootness:** If only some claims dismissed with prejudice, others may become moot during appeal
3. **Mandate:** If appeal fails, cannot refile in district court

### Mitigation Strategy
- **Brief Structure:** Lead with substantive legal error; address procedural refusal as secondary
- **Standard of Review:** Argue abuse of discretion for the underlying September 19 Order
- **Cumulative Error:** If multiple dismissals occur, aggregate into single appeal

---

## Checklist Before Choosing Strategic Surrender

- [ ] Confirm §1291 final judgment requirement blocks immediate appeal
- [ ] Confirm Rule 54(b) unavailable (claims proceeding)
- [ ] Confirm §1292(b) certification unavailable/untimely
- [ ] Assess likelihood of reversal on merits (must be >50%)
- [ ] Verify no mandatory leave to amend requirements (e.g., FRCP 11, certain civil rights statutes)
- [ ] Document the *Westinghouse* warning in the record (judge explicitly warned of prejudice)
