# Case Law Discovery

## Overview

Case law is the primary source of legal interpretation. This skill finds
relevant cases by searching the corpus for cases that interpret specific
statutes.

## Workflow

### Step 1: Query the corpus for the statute + case law

    GET http://localhost:8100/search_text?query=STATUTE+case+law+interpretation&top_k=10

### Step 2: Query the corpus for the statute + circuit + decision

    GET http://localhost:8100/search_text?query=STATUTE+CIRCUIT+circuit+decision&top_k=10

### Step 3: Query the corpus for the statute + Supreme Court

    GET http://localhost:8100/search_text?query=STATUTE+Supreme+Court+SCOTUS&top_k=10

### Step 4: Query the corpus for the statute + district court

    GET http://localhost:8100/search_text?query=STATUTE+district+court+EDPA&top_k=10

### Step 5: Cross-reference with MCP tools

- `mcp_beezle_search_case_law` — search CourtListener for case law
- `mcp_beezle_discover_authority` — combined authority discovery
- `mcp_beezle_resolve_citation` — resolve a citation to chunks

## Key patterns

### Circuit splits

- If there is a circuit split on a statute, argue for the circuit that
  is favorable to your position
- Cite the split to show the law is unsettled

### Supreme Court authority

- Always check if SCOTUS has addressed the statute
- SCOTUS decisions override all lower courts

### Circuit authority

- For federal cases, cite the circuit where the case is pending
- If no circuit authority, cite the most closely analogous circuit

### District court authority

- District court decisions are persuasive only
- Use them to show how other courts have interpreted the statute

## Example: FCRA in EDPA

1. GET /search_text?query=FCRA 15 USC 1681 case law interpretation
2. GET /search_text?query=FCRA 15 USC 1681 4th Circuit
3. GET /search_text?query=FCRA 15 USC 1681 Supreme Court
4. GET /search_text?query=FCRA 15 USC 1681 EDPA
5. Classify each: is it binding on EDPA?
6. Build the argument around binding authority

## Pitfalls

- Don't assume a case is binding — verify the court hierarchy
- Don't assume a case is still good law — check if it has been overruled
- Don't assume a case is relevant — verify it interprets the same statute
- When in doubt, query the corpus directly rather than relying on MCP tool
  defaults
