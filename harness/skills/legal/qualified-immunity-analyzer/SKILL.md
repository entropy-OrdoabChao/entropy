# Qualified Immunity Analyzer

## Overview

Judges can be sued for actions taken in their judicial capacity only if they
acted in clear absence of jurisdiction or violated clearly established law.
This skill builds those claims.

## Legal Framework

### Qualified Immunity (28 U.S.C. 1983)

Judges are entitled to qualified immunity unless:
1. They acted in clear absence of all jurisdiction
2. They violated clearly established law

### Clear Absence of Jurisdiction

- Judge acts in a case where they have no jurisdiction
- Judge issues orders beyond their authority
- Judge fails to recuse when required

### Violation of Clearly Established Law

- Judge ignores binding precedent
- Judge misapplies a statute
- Judge violates a procedural rule

### Step 3: Build the claim

For each violation, document:
- The violation (what the judge did wrong)
- The clearly established law (what the judge should have done)
- The citation (which precedent or statute establishes the law)
- The damages (what harm resulted from the violation)

## Key patterns

### Clear absence of jurisdiction

1. GET /search_text?query=JUDGE+NAME+clear+absence+jurisdiction
2. Document the jurisdictional error
3. Build the claim: Judge acted in clear absence of jurisdiction
4. Write to Obsidian: qualified_immunity_JUDGE_NAME_clear_absence.md

### Violation of clearly established law

1. GET /search_text?query=JUDGE+NAME+violation+clearly+established+law
2. Document the violation and the clearly established law
3. Build the claim: Judge violated clearly established law
4. Write to Obsidian: qualified_immunity_JUDGE_NAME_violation.md

## Pitfalls

- Don't assume a judge is acting improperly — verify the law
- Don't assume a judge is beyond jurisdiction — verify the jurisdiction
- Don't assume a judge is biased — verify the facts
- Don't build a claim without documenting the clearly established law
- Don't build a claim without documenting the damages
