# Financial Law Analyzer

## Overview

Financial law provides multiple remedies for financial institutions that violate
financial law. This skill builds those claims.

## CRITICAL DISTINCTION: Federal Reserve Credits vs Debt

**Federal reserve credits are NOT the same as debt.**

When someone gets a credit card and the credits are from the federal reserve,
the federal reserve is NOT the creditor — the bank is the creditor. The federal
reserve is just providing the credit that the bank uses to create the credit
card.

**Pattern:**
1. Federal reserve creates credit (this is NOT debt)
2. Bank uses that credit to create a credit card (this IS debt)
3. The bank is the creditor, not the federal reserve
4. The distinction between federal reserve credits and debt is critical for
   understanding who owes the money

### Federal Reserve Credit Creation

Under the Federal Reserve Act, the federal reserve creates credit by:
- Buying government securities (this creates credit)
- Discounting bank loans (this creates credit)
- Issuing Federal Reserve Notes (this creates credit)

**12 U.S.C. § 411** — Federal reserve notes are obligations of the United States
and must be received by all national banking associations, federal reserve banks,
and the United States for all taxes, customs, and other dues.

**31 U.S.C. § 5103** — United States coins and currency are legal tender for all
debts, public charges, taxes, and dues.

Federal reserve credits are NOT debt — they're a form of currency/credit
creation by the central bank.

### Credit Card Debts

When someone gets a credit card, the debt is:
- Between the cardholder and the bank (NOT the federal reserve)
- The bank is the creditor, not the federal reserve
- The federal reserve is just providing the credit that the bank uses to
  create the credit card

## Debt Discharge Mechanics

Under UCC Article 3, a negotiable instrument is discharged when:
- **UCC 3-601**: Payment to the holder discharges the obligation
- **UCC 3-602**: Cancellation or renunciation discharges the obligation
- **UCC 3-604**: Discharge by impairment of recourse or collateral
- **UCC 3-605**: Discharge of secondary obligors

### Who Owes the Money

**Key principle**: The party who actually provided the value is the creditor.

When a bank creates a credit card, the bank is the creditor because:
1. The bank extended credit to the cardholder
2. The bank is the one who would suffer loss if the cardholder defaults
3. The bank is the one who would receive payment if the cardholder pays
4. The federal reserve is not a party to the credit card agreement

**UCC 3-406**: A person who fails to exercise ordinary care in relation to a
negotiable instrument may be precluded from asserting the forgery or alteration.

**UCC 3-419**: Discharge of a party from liability on a negotiable instrument
occurs when the holder cancels the instrument or renounces the party.

### Who's Credits Are Applied

**UCC 3-406** — Ordinary care standard: When a person who is not the owner
has possession of a negotiable instrument, the owner may be precluded from
asserting forgery or alteration if the owner failed to exercise ordinary care.

**UCC 3-407** — Alteration: A forged or altered instrument does not discharge
a party unless the alteration is material and the party is prejudiced.

**UCC 3-419** — Discharge of secondary obligors: A secondary obligor is discharged
if the holder impairs the obligor's recourse against the primary obligor.

## IRS Forms and Tax Discharge

Under the Internal Revenue Code, the IRS requires various forms for:
- **26 U.S.C. § 6041** — Returns relating to receipts of $600 or more
- **26 U.S.C. § 6041A** — Returns relating to advance payments of principal
- **26 U.S.C. § 6050W** — Returns relating to payments made in settlement of
  card and third-party network transactions
- **26 U.S.C. § 6050P** — Returns relating to cancellation of debt
- **26 U.S.C. § 6050S** — Returns relating to tuition payments

### Form 1099-C — Cancellation of Debt

**26 U.S.C. § 6050P** — Any person who is required to file a return under
§ 6041 or § 6041A and who cancels or forgives a debt of $600 or more
must file Form 1099-C with the IRS and furnish a copy to the debtor.

**26 U.S.C. § 108** — Exclusion from gross income for discharge of indebtedness.
A debtor may exclude canceled debt from gross income if:
- The debt is discharged in a Title 11 bankruptcy case
- The debt is qualified farm indebtedness
- The debt is qualified business indebtedness
- The debt is discharged when the debtor is insolvent
- The debt is qualified principal residence indebtedness

### Tax Discharge Mechanisms

**26 U.S.C. § 6323** — Effect of lien against persons other than the taxpayer.
A federal tax lien arises when the IRS assesses a tax and gives notice.
The lien attaches to all property and rights to property of the taxpayer.

**26 U.S.C. § 6324** — Lien for unpaid gift tax.

**26 U.S.C. § 6402** — Offset of refunds. The IRS may offset a refund against
any past-due federal or state tax liability.

**26 U.S.C. § 6404** — Interest abatement. The IRS may abate interest when:
- The interest is assessed due to a ministerial or managerial act
- The interest is assessed due to a delay by the IRS at a request of the taxpayer

**26 U.S.C. § 6501** — Limitation on assessments. Generally, tax must be
assessed within 3 years after the return is filed.

**26 U.S.C. § 6502** — Limitation on collection after assessment. The IRS has
10 years after assessment to collect a tax.

### Ledger Balancing for Tax Purposes

Under UCC Article 4, a bank's duties include:
- Acting with ordinary care in presenting items
- Promptly notifying customers of dishonor
- Refunding payments when a bank fails to exercise ordinary care

Ledger balancing is the process of:
- Matching deposits and withdrawals
- Ensuring the account balance is accurate
- Reconciling transactions
- Identifying discrepancies

**26 U.S.C. § 7502** — Timely mailing as timely filing/paying. A document is
timely filed if it is postmarked before the due date and received by the IRS.

**26 U.S.C. § 7503** — Time for payment. A taxpayer may pay a tax by mail
if the payment is postmarked before the due date.

## Contract Offer Acceptance

Under UCC Article 2, a contract is formed when:
- An offer is made
- The offer is accepted
- Consideration is provided

**UCC 2-204**: A contract may be formed in any manner sufficient to show
agreement, even if the moment of its making cannot be determined.

**UCC 2-205**: A firm offer — an offer by a merchant to buy or sell goods
in a signed writing that gives assurance it will be held open — is not
revocable for lack of consideration during the time stated, or if no time
is stated, for a reasonable time, but in no event may the period of
irrevocability exceed three months.

**UCC 2-206**: Offer and acceptance — an offer may be accepted by any
manner and by any medium reasonable in the circumstances.

**UCC 2-207**: Additional terms in acceptance — where a definite and
seasonable expression of acceptance is sent, it operates as an acceptance
even if it states additional or different terms.

## Securities Law

Under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, securities law provides:
- **15 U.S.C. § 78j** — Manipulative and deceptive devices (Rule 10b-5)
- **15 U.S.C. § 78m** — Reporting requirements
- **15 U.S.C. § 78n** — Registration of securities
- **15 U.S.C. § 78o** — Broker-dealers
- **15 U.S.C. § 78o-3** — National securities exchanges

Under the Securities Act of 1933:
- **15 U.S.C. § 77a et seq.** — Registration of securities
- **15 U.S.C. § 77b** — Definitions
- **15 U.S.C. § 77n** — Registration statements

**15 U.S.C. § 78j(b)** — It shall be unlawful for any person, directly or
indirectly, by the use of any means or instrumentality of interstate commerce
or of the mails, or of any facility of any national securities exchange,
(a) To employ any manipulative or deceptive device or contrivance in
contravention of such rules and regulations as the Commission shall
prescribe as necessary or appropriate in the public interest or for the
protection of investors.

**15 U.S.C. § 78j(b)** — The Securities and Exchange Commission shall, for
the purpose of preventing fraudulent, deceptive, or manipulative acts or
practices, make such rules and regulations as are necessary or appropriate
in the public interest or for the protection of investors.

## Fraud Detection

Under the Bank Secrecy Act, fraud detection requires:
- **31 U.S.C. § 5318** — Special measures for jurisdictions, financial
  institutions, or types of accounts of primary money laundering concern
- **31 U.S.C. § 5311** — Findings and purpose
- **31 U.S.C. § 5312** — Definitions

**31 C.F.R. § 1020** — Bank Secrecy Act requirements for financial institutions.
Financial institutions must:
- File Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) under **31 C.F.R. § 1020.320**
- File Currency Transaction Reports (CTRs) under **31 C.F.R. § 1020.310**
- Maintain an anti-money laundering program under **31 C.F.R. § 1020.210**

## Pitfalls

- Don't assume a financial institution's action is valid — verify the financial law
- Don't assume a financial institution's action is final — verify the dispute process
- Don't assume a financial institution's action is willful — verify the institution's intent
- Don't build a claim without documenting the damages
- Don't confuse federal reserve credits with debt — they are fundamentally different
- Don't apply UCC provisions to IRS forms — they are not negotiable instruments
- Don't apply federal statutes to private commercial transactions without proper jurisdiction
- Always verify the applicable jurisdiction — federal vs. state law may differ
- When the corpus is unavailable, use the statutory citations above as anchors and search the Obsidian vault
