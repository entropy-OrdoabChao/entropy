# Insurance Law Analyzer

## Overview

Insurance law provides multiple remedies for insurers who fail to provide
coverage, deny claims, or breach the duty of good faith. This skill builds
those claims.

## Key Insurance Concepts

### Coverage E — First-Party Coverage

Coverage E is first-party property coverage under a homeowners insurance policy.
It covers:
- The dwelling (the structure itself)
- Other structures on the property
- Personal property
- Loss of use (additional living expenses)

### Duty of Good Faith and Fair Dealing

Every insurance policy contains an implied duty of good faith and fair dealing.
When an insurer breaches this duty, the insured may recover:
- Compensatory damages for the breach
- Consequential damages for losses caused by the breach
- Punitive damages for willful breaches

### Bad Faith Insurance Claims

Under 42 Pa.C.S. § 8371 (Pennsylvania), an insured may recover:
- Compensatory damages for the breach of the duty of good faith
- Punitive damages for willful breaches
- Attorney's fees and costs

### Coverage Denial

When an insurer denies coverage, the insured may challenge the denial by:
- Showing the insurer's interpretation of the policy is wrong
- Showing the insurer's interpretation conflicts with the plain language
- Showing the insurer's interpretation is contrary to established law

2. Document the bad faith
3. Build the claim: Insurer breached the duty of good faith

### Coverage breach

1. GET /search_text?query=insurance+coverage+breach+duty
2. Document the breach
3. Build the claim: Insurer breached the coverage
4. Write to Obsidian: insurance_claim_coverage_breach.md

## Pitfalls

- Don't assume a denial is valid — verify the policy language
- Don't assume a denial is final — verify the appeal process
- Don't assume a breach is willful — verify the insurer's intent
- Don't build a claim without documenting the damages
