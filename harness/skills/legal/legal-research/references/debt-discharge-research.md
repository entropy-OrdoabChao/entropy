## 1. Overview of Debt Discharge Mechanisms

|-----------|-------------|--------------|

## 2. UCC Article 3 — Negotiable Instruments (§ 3-104)

A negotiable instrument must:
- Be in writing and signed by the maker or drawer
- Contain an unconditional promise or order to pay a fixed amount of money
- Be payable on demand or at a definite time
- Be payable to bearer or to order
- Contain no other undertaking beyond payment

**Common Examples:** Promissory notes, checks, certificates of deposit, drafts (including cashier's checks)

---

## 3. Holder in Due Course (HDC) Doctrine (§ 3-302)

A holder in due course is a holder who takes an instrument:
1. **For Value:** Gives something of value
2. **In Good Faith:** Honesty in fact AND observance of reasonable commercial standards
3. **Without Notice:** No notice of overdue/dishonored instrument, unauthorized signature, alteration, claim, or defense

**Critical Rule:** HDC status shields the instrument from most defenses.

| Defense Type | Against HDC? | Against Non-HDC? |
|--------------|--------------|------------------|
| Personal defenses (breach of contract, fraud in inducement) | ❌ No | ✅ Yes |
| Real defenses (forgery, infancy, duress, discharge in bankruptcy) | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes |

**UCC § 3-302(b):** Notice of discharge of a party, other than discharge in an insolvency proceeding, is not notice of a defense under subsection (a), but **discharge is effective against a person who became a holder in due course with notice of the discharge.**

## 4. Effect of Instrument on Underlying Obligation (§ 3-310)

### (a) Certified Check, Cashier's Check, or Teller's Check

**The obligation is DISCHARGED immediately** — same as if cash were paid. No suspension.

### (b) Note or Uncertified Check

**The obligation is SUSPENDED** (not discharged) pending resolution:
- **(b)(1) Uncertified check:** Suspension until dishonor or payment/certification
- **(b)(2) Note:** Suspension until dishonor or payment
- **(b)(3) Dishonor:** Creditor may enforce either the instrument or the obligation
- **(b)(4) Third-party instrument:** Creditor may NOT enforce the obligation to the extent suspended

### (c) Other Instruments

- (i) Subsection (a) if instrument has bank as maker/acceptor
- (ii) Subsection (b) otherwise

## 5. Accord and Satisfaction by Use of Instrument (§ 3-311)

### Requirements (proving accord and satisfaction):
1. **Good Faith Tender:** Tendered instrument as full satisfaction of claim
2. **Disputed Claim:** Amount was unliquidated or subject to bona fide dispute
3. **Payment Obtained:** Claimant obtained payment of the instrument
4. **Conspicuous Statement:** Instrument or accompanying communication contained conspicuous statement that instrument was tendered as full satisfaction

### Exceptions (claim NOT discharged):
1. **Organization Exception:** Claimant sent conspicuous statement designating a specific office for disputed debts, and instrument was not received there
2. **90-Day Repayment:** Claimant tendered repayment within 90 days of payment (does not apply if claimant is an organization with designated office)

### Discharge by Knowledge (§ 3-311(d)):
A claim is discharged if the claimant (or agent with direct responsibility) knew the instrument was tendered in full satisfaction before collection was initiated.

## 6. Discharge of Instrument Obligations (§§ 3-601, 3-602)

### UCC § 3-601: Discharge and Effect of Discharge
- **(a)** Obligation discharged by payment per instrument terms, modification, voluntary discharge, or waiver
- **(b)** Discharge is **NOT effective against HDC without notice of the discharge**

### UCC § 3-602: Payment
- **(a)** Payment by or on behalf of obligor to person entitled to enforce → obligation discharged
- **(b)** Note payment to former obligee requires adequate notification of transfer
- **(e)** Obligation NOT discharged if:
  - (1) Claim under § 3-306 is enforceable and payor has knowledge of injunction or accepted indemnity; or
  - (2) Payor knows instrument is stolen and pays person in wrongful possession

---

## 7. Instruments Signed for Accommodation (§ 3-419)

- **(b)** Accommodation party is obliged to pay in the capacity in which they sign
- **(c)** Anomalous indorsement or surety/guarantor words → presumption of accommodation
- **(d)** Guarantee of **collection** → only liable after resort to accommodated party
- **(e)** Guarantee of **payment** → liable in same circumstances as accommodated party
- **(f)** Accommodation party who pays is entitled to reimbursement from accommodated party

## 8. IRS Form 1099-C and Debt Cancellation

### General Rule: Discharged debt is TAXABLE INCOME (IRC § 61(a)(12))

### Exceptions (Not Taxable):
- Bankruptcy discharge (IRC § 108(a)(1)(A))
- Insolvency (IRC § 108(a)(1)(B))
- Qualified farm debt (IRC § 108(a)(1)(C))
- Qualified real property business debt (IRC § 108(a)(1)(D))
- Mortgage debt on principal residence under $2M (IRC § 108(a)(1)(E))
- Student loan discharge under certain programs
- Discharge in Title 11 bankruptcy case

### Filing Requirements (Treas. Reg. § 1.6050P-1):
| Requirement | Details |
|-------------|---------|
| Threshold | Debt discharged ≥ $600 |
| Deadline to debtor | January 31 |
| Deadline to IRS | February 28 |
| Information | Creditor name, amount, date, debtor TIN |

## 10. Practical Applications

### Scenario A: Medical Debt Settlement
1. Hospital agrees to accept $5,000 (was $20,000)
2. Patient pays $5,000 marked "full settlement"
3. Hospital accepts
4. Result: Accord and satisfaction
5. Challenge: Hospital files 1099-C for $15,000
6. Defense: Insolvency or hardship exemption

### Scenario B: Certified Check Payment
1. Debtor owes $50,000 on promissory note
2. Debtor pays with cashier's check
3. Under UCC § 3-310(a), obligation is immediately discharged
4. Creditor refuses to return note, continues collection
5. Defense: UCC § 3-310(a) — obligation discharged

### Scenario C: Uncertified Check Payment
1. Debtor owes $50,000 on promissory note
2. Debtor pays with personal check
3. Under UCC § 3-310(b), obligation is SUSPENDED (not discharged)
4. Check is dishonored
5. Under UCC § 3-310(b)(3), creditor may enforce EITHER the check OR the obligation

### Scenario D: Bankruptcy Discharge
1. Debtor files Chapter 7 bankruptcy
2. Court discharges debt
3. Creditor files 1099-C anyway
4. Defense: Bankruptcy discharge (11 U.S.C. § 524)

## Key Takeaways

1. **UCC § 3-310 is the critical bridge**: certified/cashier's/teller's checks DISCHARGE immediately; notes/uncertified checks only SUSPEND
2. **UCC § 3-311 requires**: conspicuous "full satisfaction" statement AND bona fide dispute
3. **UCC § 3-601(b)**: discharge is NOT effective against HDC without notice
4. **UCC § 3-602(e)**: payment does NOT discharge if a claim under § 3-306 is enforceable and the payor has knowledge
5. **HDC status is powerful** — most defenses don't apply against HDC holders
6. **1099-C reporting has exceptions** — bankruptcy, insolvency, qualified principal residence
7. **Documentation is critical** for all debt discharge claims
