# Authority Hierarchy Builder

## Overview

Not all authorities are equal. This skill maps the hierarchy to determine
which authorities are binding and which are merely persuasive.

## Federal Court Hierarchy

### Supreme Court
- BINDING on ALL federal courts
- BINDING on ALL state courts on federal law questions

### Circuit Court of Appeals
- BINDING on district courts within that circuit
- Persuasive (not binding) on other circuits

### District Court
- Persuasive only (never binding)
- Even within the same district, one judge is not bound by another

## State Court Hierarchy (Pennsylvania)

### Supreme Court
- BINDING on ALL PA courts
- BINDING on PA lower courts on state law questions

### Superior Court
- BINDING on PA trial courts within that district
- Persuasive on other districts

### Commonwealth Court
- BINDING on PA trial courts within that district
- Persuasive on other districts

### Trial Court (CCP)
- Persuasive only (never binding)

## Statutory Hierarchy

### Constitution
- BINDING on ALL courts and agencies
- Highest authority — cannot be overridden by statute

### Federal Statute (USC)
- BINDING on ALL federal courts
- BINDING on state courts on federal law questions
- Supersedes conflicting state law (Supremacy Clause)

### Federal Regulation (CFR)
- BINDING if properly promulgated under a statute
- Can be challenged if beyond statutory authority

### State Statute
- BINDING on state courts
- Persuasive on federal courts (on state law questions)

### State Regulation
- BINDING if properly promulgated under a statute
- Can be challenged if beyond statutory authority

## Workflow

### Step 1: Identify the authorities cited

List all authorities cited in the filing or opposing counsel's argument.

### Step 2: Classify each authority

For each authority, determine:
- Issuing court (for case law)
- Binding on the court where the case is pending?
- Mandatory or persuasive?

### Step 3: Query the corpus for binding authority

    GET http://localhost:8100/search_text?query=STATUTE+binding+precedent&top_k=10

### Step 4: Cross-reference with MCP tools

- `mcp_beezle_is_binding_on_court` — determine whether a cited court binds
  a target court under stare decisis
- `mcp_beezle_classify_citation` — classify a citation by its issuing court
- `mcp_beezle_check_definition_scope` — classify a statutory definition as
  closed ("means") or expansive ("includes")

## Example: FCRA case in EDPA

1. Authorities cited: 988 F.2d 476 (9th Cir.), Loftis v. UPS (6th Cir.)
2. Classify: 988 F.2d 476 = 9th Circuit (not binding on EDPA)
3. Classify: Loftis v. UPS = 6th Circuit (not binding on EDPA)
4. Query corpus for EDPA binding authority on FCRA:
   GET /search_text?query=EDPA FCRA binding precedent
5. Establish that only 4th Circuit or SCOTUS authority is binding on EDPA

## Pitfalls

- Don't assume a circuit decision is binding on a different circuit
- Don't assume a district court decision is binding on another district
- Don't assume a state court decision is binding on a federal court
- Always verify the court hierarchy — a "Circuit Court" may be a state court,
  not a federal Circuit Court
- When in doubt, query the corpus for the specific court's binding authority
